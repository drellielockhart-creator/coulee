### What changed

1. **The needs ledger no longer copies the actor table** (`needs/system.ts`, `needs/tactical.ts`, `activity/api.ts`). This was the O(n²) hotspot.
   - `hourlyLedger` split a walker's hour, and a journey's moving/waiting interval, by recursing on `{...world, activity: {...byActor, [id]: …}}`. That copied every actor's activity row once per walker, per need, per hour.
   - The tactical frame's walking rate made the same copy per actor.
   - The ledger now takes an optional `doing` row (the actor's activity as if it were this one) and reads it in place of `byActor[id]`. `laborDoing` and `asleepDoing` are `laborOf` and `isAsleep` for a given activity id, and those two delegate to them.
   - No other callee reads `world.activity`, so this is exact. It also removes the inline-cache sensitivity the food-forecast pass saw (the needs system at 296 ms/h after a deopt).
   - County needs: 10.8 → 7.3 ms/h on its own.
2. **One inventory index per hour** (`sim/sim.ts`). The hourly systems now run inside one `withInventoryPass`.
   - Every stack creation, move, hoist and regional commit goes through `items/api`, which extends the pass index or drops it. Every consumer checks deletions live. So each system reads exactly what a fresh index would give.
   - Before, each system without its own pass rebuilt the index from all stacks: `heatPlaces` and the needs ledger (twice), activity, and the first call inside each module's own pass. The worst case was `chronicle/facts.ts carriedTenths`: it reads one `readinessOf` per member of a short household, and each one rebuilt the index three times (`holderStacks`, `kitchenOf`, `waterAvailable`).
   - At county that was about 29 builds of about 4,700 stacks per hour. It is now about one, plus rebuilds after moves.
   - Chronicle at county: 5.4 → 1.9 ms/h.
3. **Index-backed `tryMerge`** (`items/api.ts`). This sped up stack creation, which used to scan every stack in the world.
   - Inside a pass with a live index, the first compatible stack in ascending id order is the first one in the index's list for that location, or else among the ids created since the index was built.
   - Non-stackable defs return at once.
   - `replenishDomestic` at county: about 2.7% → about 0.1%.
4. **Rota grouping** (`activity/calendar.ts ensureCalendar`). Rota candidates are grouped by household once instead of filtered per household. This was O(households × candidates) per rota block per day. Order is preserved.
5. **Small exact items:**
   - `boardMeal` returns before `keeperAt`'s scan of every actor when the place has no meal service (`provisions/services.ts`).
   - `processingActorIds` asks each site's ownership once per call (`region/processing.ts`).
   - `heatPlaces` hoists the sorted hearth list out of its per-place loop (`needs/thermal.ts`).

## Part 2: a return to full rations is judged at full rations (behavior change)

**Decision (Ellie, 2026-10-14):** a household on short rations is judged at full rations before it goes back to full. It is logged in the 00 decisions log.

**Before:** restore when the forecast at the *cut* ration reached 30 days. A household could be restored to full and cut again the next day once the full-ration forecast dropped under 21 days.

**Now** (`provisions/household.ts decideRations`):
- On short rations, with no further cut due and the cut-ration forecast at or above 30 days, a second forecast runs at full rations: `planReadiness(..., atRationPct = 100)`, horizon 31.
- Full rations are restored only if that forecast also reaches 30 days.
- The cut-ration forecast always lasts at least as long, so it gates the second projection.
- `planReadiness` gains an optional `atRationPct`. It is used for the daily need, the projected plates and the projection's household copy. Default behavior is unchanged.
- A refused restore leaves the ration in place, so the cut ration's standing forecast can stand. It breaks the first morning the larder gains food.

**Readout (P1):**
- The new optional state `HouseholdProvisions.rationRestore {day, fullDaysTenths}` records the refused restore, as integers.
- `Readiness.rations` Explain adds three terms: "Forecast at full rations made on day", "Days of food at full rations then, tenths", and "Days at full rations a restore needs".
- The warning line reads: "Rations stay at 75%: on <date> the larder would have lasted 24.0 days at full rations, short of the 30 a return to full rations needs."
- The "Full rations restored" Explain now carries both figures: cut ration and full rations.
- The field is cleared by any decision that does not refuse a restore, and on days with no decision.

**Replay rules version 5 → 6** (`packages/play/src/session.ts`). The pin is in `brawl.test.ts`.

**Tests** (`packages/cli/test/food-forecast.test.ts`, "a return to full rations is judged at full rations"):
- A 75% household whose larder covers 30 days at 75% but not at full rations stays at 75%. Its `rationRestore` and readout name the full-ration figure, and no `provisions.rations` event fires. This test fails under the old rule.
- A 75% household whose larder covers 30 days at full rations is restored. The restore Explain shows ≥ 30 days at full rations, and the household is not cut at the next day start.

## Part 3: a refused restore is a shopping trigger (follow-up to part 2)

### The hypothesis was wrong; the gap is real

**The part-2 hypothesis is false.** I traced households 11 and 5 in gen:town 19 day by day from day 270 (`runs/scratch/pn-winter.ts`, `pn-inflow.ts`, `pn-store.ts`):
- Neither sat at 90% with a refused restore. Both were on **full** rations until real shortages in days 312–321.
- The shopping trigger was never judged at the cut ration. Both the urge (`api.ts`) and the buying step (`economy.ts`) already compare the larder with full-ration daily need.
- Both households had **zero cash and zero purse** all winter. Their tab at the well-stocked store (place 26) was at its credit limit (2,400¢). The other store (place 18), where they still had credit, was empty on most days.
- In the pre-part-2 run, household 11 happened to shop on day 316, a day when store 18 was stocked. In the part-2 run its larder was still above the 21-day trigger that day, and it missed the restock.
- So the March drop is trajectory-specific in a cash-starved town. Re-running the **same town** (world 19) with sim seeds 20 and 21 shows no winter regression under part 2:

### The change (P8: the same planner; P2: no controller branch; integers)

- `rules.ts`: `shoppingDays(month, restoreRefused)`.
  - New rule `SHOPPING.restoreTopUpDays = 32`.
  - A household with a refused restore (`rationRestore` set, below full) shops at trigger `max(trigger, 32)` toward target `max(target, 32)`.
  - Days are counted against full-ration daily need, as before.
  - 32 = the 30-day restore threshold plus two days for preparation losses.
- `household.ts`: `restoreRefused(world, hid)`.
- `api.ts` (the shopping urge) and `economy.ts` (the buying step) use it.
  - The errand has its own legible label when only the refusal asks for it: "Rations stay short until the larder lasts at full rations: buy provisions" (reason "Short rations held: provision duty").
- **Test.** The 75% stays-cut test now also checks that, in store hours, the head gets that shopping urge although the larder is above 21 days. It fails without the trigger.

## Part 4: review fixes (on `0fb9549`)

**MED-1: the top-up and the restore now use one measure.**

*The problem.* Part 3 sent a refused household shopping until its raw larder (`larderKcal`) reached 32 days of full-ration need. But the restore asks the P8 planner at full rations for 30 days. So a larder of produce that spoils, or flour the household cannot cook, could reach 32 raw days and still be refused at a planner figure near 27. That household then got no errand until it ate back under 32 raw days.

*The fix.* The refusal now records the larder it was judged on: `rationRestore.larderKcal`, an optional integer. The top-up target (`household.ts restoreTopUpKcal`) is that larder scaled by what the forecast made of it:

`target = ceil(32 × larderKcal × 10 / fullDaysTenths)` kcal

- This is the raw larder that the refusing forecast says would last 32 days at full rations.
- It is capped at 60 days of need (`SHOPPING.restoreTopUpCapDays`).
- Both the urge (`api.ts`) and the buying step (`economy.ts`) compare the same raw `larderKcal` with it. Buying adds `target − larder` to the kcal order.
- The shopping plan's Explain gains a "Top-up toward full rations, kcal" term.
- A save from before this change has no `larderKcal`; it falls back to 32 days of need.

*Stuck households.* "Stuck" means a refused restore with no errand that could fix it: before this fix, a raw larder of at least 32 days; after it, a larder at or above its own target. Counts are household-days sampled at day end, with `pn-gap.ts`.

## Method
