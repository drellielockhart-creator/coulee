1855-04-01 00:00 The village was right, for the wrong reasons. Hester Fenn was of the hidden folk, and had been all along. It was not the rumors that found it out; it was coincidence, and a cow that died at the right time.

1855-04-01 00:00 Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.
1855-04-01 00:00 We are sorry to report that the Newcomer lodgingses are in want, and have been for a day. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.
1855-04-01 00:00 It has been a day. The ration is at 50 in the hundred and the children no longer ask. That is the worst of it: they no longer ask.

1855-04-02 00:00 Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.
1855-04-02 06:00 Nora Dunleavy was in close talk with me at Dunleavy's Tavern on Monday at first light. I would give a good deal to know the subject.

1855-04-02 14:00 We are glad to report that Mr. Josiah Trevenna has paid Mr. L. Vance what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.
1855-04-02 15:00 The debt was repaid on a Tuesday, without ceremony. It had been, for Gussie Pratt, a question of whether Lemuel Hatch could be trusted; it was now a question of whether the answer mattered.
1855-04-02 17:00 It was Johann Brenner who was said to be at fault. It was Hester Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.
1855-04-02 19:00 A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.
1855-04-03 00:00 Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.
1855-04-03 07:00 Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Captain Trevenna had been through them all in two days.
