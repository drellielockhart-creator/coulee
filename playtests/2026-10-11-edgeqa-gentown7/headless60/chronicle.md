# Hazel Ford — seed 7

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern.*

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Keller, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. Johann Strauss saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Miss R. Carter was seen carrying relief to the door of Miss Sarah Vance, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**A Timely Kindness** Master T. Trevenna, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Master K. Strauss saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that the Cranes' cows went dry overnight. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

**In Want** We regret to state that the Strausses are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Miss J. Keller was seen carrying relief to the door of Miss W. Brenner, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. Johann Strauss, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Rev. A. Vik saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Ridge Lutheran Church and eight to Crane General Store and Outfitter. Court day and market day were kept.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a last quarter, and the ford quiet.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mrs. H. Vance was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. E. Brenner, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Miss R. Carter has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

**A Timely Kindness** Miss R. Carter, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Miss W. Brenner saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of the Strausses is over; they held. The first ramps came, and the pig was a blessing, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**Uneasiness on the Ford** Mr. Silas Vance is much talked of, and little spoken to. "Silas Vance is of the hidden folk" is the burden of the talk. We counsel patience, and the keeping of the peace, and expect to be listened to by neither side.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that a fever took hold of a child in the Kellers' house. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-five to Ridge Lutheran Church and six to Crane General Store and Outfitter. Market day was kept.

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mrs. H. Carter was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. Silas Vance, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing crescent.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Sheehan house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

**A Poor Return** Mr. Silas Vance is reported to have had a poor return for a kindness. Mrs. H. Vance insulted Mr. Silas Vance at Brenner West Cove Farm on Sunday in the afternoon, two weeks after being helped by him. Memory in this county is long and not always grateful.

**Betrayed** It pains us to record that Mrs. H. Vance, who stood by Mr. Silas Vance two weeks ago, has been wronged by the very person helped: Mr. Silas Vance came to blows with Mrs. H. Vance at Brenner West Cove Farm on Sunday in the afternoon. The village is shocked, or says so.

**A Fight at Brenner West Cove Farm** Blows were exchanged at Brenner West Cove Farm on Sunday in the afternoon: Mr. Silas Vance against Mrs. H. Vance. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

**The Talk of the County** The tale that "a fever took hold of a child in the Kellers' house" has grown in the telling, as tales do. It has crossed the river and come back larger.

**A Timely Kindness** Miss J. Sheehan, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. Silas Crane saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Poor Aim at Vance Upper Coulee Farm** Mrs. H. Vance aimed a hard word at Miss Sarah Vance at Vance Upper Coulee Farm and hit nothing; Miss Sarah Vance was seen to smile, which is worse.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

**In Want** The Vances are in want. It pains us to record it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Cranes' cows went dry overnight" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**Through the Worst** We are glad to report that the Vances are through the worst of it. The ration is restored and the barrel, if not full, is no longer the subject of prayer.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It is our unpleasant duty to note that someone has been at the Nordbys' smokehouse. The neighbors are doing what neighbors do.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Thirteen matters of note this week. Posted at the store: dry beans at 4¢ the lb and potatoes at 1¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-six to Ridge Lutheran Church and four to Crane General Store and Outfitter. Market day was kept.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. H. Carter has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**The Carter-Keller Difficulty** Between the Carters and the Kellers the quarrel has taken root. It began, as such things do, in a small matter, and has since grown a large family of its own.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** The village was right, for the wrong reasons. Silas Vance was of the hidden folk, and had been all along. It was not the rumors that found it out; it was coincidence, and a cow that died at the right time.

**April 3.** The louder Silas Vance spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 4.** Behind the door of the Whitcomb house: three days of potatoes, dried apples and cornmeal, 0 cold meals, and nothing worth the naming lost to the damp. The village saw a quiet family and took it for a contented one.
