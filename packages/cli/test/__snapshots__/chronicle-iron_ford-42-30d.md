# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**Accounts Settled** It is agreeable to relate that Miss H. Fenn has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**A Fight at Penhallow Diggings** A fight took place at Penhallow Diggings on Friday in the forenoon between Mr. Jory Trevenna and Mr. Josiah Trevenna. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mr. Jory Trevenna left the field.

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dry beans at 4¢ the lb and potatoes at 1¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

Mr. J. Brenner threatened Mr. L. Vance at Dunleavy's Tavern on Monday of an evening, and the village is not slow to remark it.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; eight are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

Several persons at Dunleavy's Tavern report the following: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** A scuffle at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening: Mrs. I. Tveit swung, and Mr. J. Brenner did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** We regret to state that Miss L. Pratt struck at Miss H. Fenn at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Saturday in the forenoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

**Accounts Settled** Mr. E. Fenn has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

A curious report reaches us from Glover Farm: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "ordinary causes account for the signs at Dunleavy's Tavern" has reached every house on Main Street, and some on Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew sixteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing crescent; work laid by.*

**Talk** Whispers have begun to go about concerning Mrs. N. Dunleavy. We do not repeat them. We observe only that they are going.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. Three taps come from the stone after the last hammer-stroke.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It grieves us to say that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing gibbous, and the ford quiet.*

**Accounts Settled** It is agreeable to relate that Miss H. Fenn has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Ferryman's House in the weekly register: A loop of red thread hangs from the pantry latch.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** It was Ingrid Tveit who was said to be at fault. It was Nora Dunleavy who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 1.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ezra Sloat had been through them all in a day.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** The debt was repaid on a Tuesday, without ceremony. It had been, for Lucius Vance, a question of whether Captain Trevenna could be trusted; it was now a question of whether the answer mattered.

**April 1.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Gussie Pratt had been through them all in a day.

**April 2.** Hester Fenn could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Demelza Pascoe. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 788 meals eaten, 103 cold, about 2424 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, fine milled flour and potatoes. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** Johann Brenner had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Twenty defenses in six days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 8.** Repression, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ingrid Tveit had been through them all in three days.

**April 8.** Michael Dunleavy never told anyone what the kindness cost in time and trouble.

**April 8.** A mind at its seams: repression again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Edwin Fenn was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 9.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 9.** Minnie Fenn was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Eight defenses in eight days, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 10.** Hester Fenn had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Ten defenses in nine days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 11.** The louder Hester Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 782 meals (80 cold); stores ran short not once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 3

**April 15.** The louder Hester Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 15.** The story needed no malice; it needed only seven people each willing to be the one who knew. The truth, whatever it was, was never consulted.

**April 15.** Of the seventeen good turns Captain Trevenna did in thirteen days, not one was mentioned at the store. That was the point of them.

**April 17.** A mind at its seams: projection again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Johanne Moe was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 18.** It was Lydia Pratt who was said to be at fault. It was Johanne Moe who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 18.** Hester Fenn could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Demelza Pascoe. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 19.** Pastor Tveit was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Fourteen defenses in three weeks, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 19.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Loveday Trevenna was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 21.** Behind the doors this week: 791 meals eaten, 77 cold, about 2470 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, potatoes and fine milled flour. Spoiled and wasted: clabber and whole milk.

### Week 4

**April 22.** It cost Hester Fenn more to pay than the sum was worth, and Gussie Pratt knew it, and took the money anyway out of respect.

**April 23.** It was Johann Brenner who was said to be at fault. It was Pastor Tveit who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 23.** Demelza Pascoe never told anyone what the kindness cost in time and trouble.

**April 24.** Karl Brenner was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Five defenses in three weeks, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 25.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 25.** The louder Pastor Tveit spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 25.** Bartholomew Pascoe had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in two weeks, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 26.** Michael Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Michael Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 28.** Week 4, as the larders knew it: 777 meals (61 cold); stores ran short three times; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.
