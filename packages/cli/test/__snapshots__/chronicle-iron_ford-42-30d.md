# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mr. M. Dunleavy was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Bell, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. M. Brenner saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

**A Debt Pressed** We are informed that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mrs. L. Trevenna was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. J. Pike, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. E. Fenn, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. Josiah Trevenna saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** It is agreeable to relate that Mrs. D. Pascoe was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

**A Timely Kindness** Miss C. Stoughton, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. M. Bell saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

Several persons at Penhallow Row report the following: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Blows were exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday of an evening: Mr. L. Vance against Mr. J. Brenner. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

**The Brenner-Dunleavy Difficulty** Between the Brenners and the Dunleavys the quarrel has taken root. It began, as such things do, in a small matter, and has since grown a large family of its own.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Fenn Farm: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

A curious report reaches us from Dunleavy's Tavern: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

**A Timely Kindness** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. J. Pike was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. A. Whitcomb saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** We are sorry to report that Mrs. A. Pratt struck at Mr. J. Brenner at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Friday at noon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew nine to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

**Talk** It is the common report that Mrs. N. Dunleavy is not altogether what she seems. The loafers are specific on nothing and certain of everything.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Moe Farm. The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. Josiah Trevenna has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. A. Whitcomb has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. J. Pike has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

Our readers may judge the account from Dunleavy's Tavern. A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

**A Timely Kindness** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. J. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

**A Poor Aim at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. J. Brenner attempted to put an insult upon Mr. M. Dunleavy at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening, but misjudged the room. The laugh, we hear, went the other way.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**Accounts Settled** Mrs. M. Fenn has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing gibbous; work laid by.*

**All Over the Ford** The story that "Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Glover Farm in the weekly register: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*
