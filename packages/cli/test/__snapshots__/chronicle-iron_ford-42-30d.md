# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

Several persons at Penhallow Row report the following: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

A plain-spoken difference passed between Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. L. Vance at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday of an evening; Mr. J. Brenner threatened Mr. L. Vance before others.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Three matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Fenn Farm: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

A curious report reaches us from Glover Farm: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is our unpleasant duty to note that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

> *The Almanac.* Three matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Moe Farm. The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. A second footfall sounds from inside the ledge.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** A scuffle at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening: Mr. J. Pike swung, and Mr. J. Brenner did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**A Debt Pressed** We hear from a respectable source that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Mrs. M. Fenn for $2.40. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**Accounts Settled** Mrs. M. Fenn has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

**Accounts Settled** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. E. Fenn has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

**Accounts Settled** Mr. Jory Trevenna has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing gibbous, and the ford quiet.*

**Talk** It is said about the village that Mr. J. Brenner is not altogether what he seems. The loafers are specific on nothing and certain of everything.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. D. Pascoe has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Glover Farm in the weekly register: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** It grieves us to say that Mr. T. Glover struck at Mr. J. Brenner at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Monday at noon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

**Hard Words at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** Mr. J. Brenner insulted Mrs. A. Pratt to the face at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Monday at noon. The company took sides, and the story was round the village by supper.
