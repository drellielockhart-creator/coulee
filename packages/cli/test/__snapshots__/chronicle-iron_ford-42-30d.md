# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mrs. M. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Bell, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. M. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Debt Pressed** Mrs. A. Pratt has pressed Mr. J. Brenner for $6.40, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

**Accounts Settled** Mr. J. Brenner has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

A sharp exchange passed between Mr. J. Brenner and Mrs. A. Pratt at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Sunday in the afternoon; Mr. J. Brenner threatened Mrs. A. Pratt before others.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**A Brawl at Brenner's Smithy** Mr. K. Brenner sought a fight with Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner's Smithy on Tuesday in the forenoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. K. Brenner had planned.

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**In Want** We regret to state that the Whitcombs are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: dry beans at 4¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Court day and market day were kept.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mrs. H. Glover was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. E. Fenn, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a last quarter, and the ford quiet.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. Josiah Trevenna, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. T. Glover saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Brenner has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of the Whitcombs is over; they held. The first ramps came, and the pig was a blessing, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** We regret to state that Mrs. A. Pratt struck at Mr. J. Brenner at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Tuesday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. K. Brenner were seen to quarrel at Brenner House on Thursday at first light; the matter was a grave one, or looked it.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

**A Poor Aim at Brenner House** Mr. J. Brenner had a speech ready for Mr. K. Brenner at Brenner House and got through half of it. The rest was lost to laughter, at his own expense.

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home.*

**A Poor Return** Mr. J. Brenner is reported to have had a poor return for a kindness. Mr. K. Brenner insulted Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon, seven days after being helped by him. Memory in this county is long and not always grateful.

**A Fight at Brenner House** Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. K. Brenner settled a difference with their fists at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon. Mr. J. Brenner was the settler.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Bell has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Poor Aim at Brenner House** Mr. K. Brenner aimed a hard word at Mrs. M. Brenner at Brenner House and hit nothing; Mrs. M. Brenner was seen to smile, which is worse.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mr. A. Whitcomb was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Timely Kindness** Miss B. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Miss L. Pratt saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**In Want** The Stoughtons are in want. It grieves us to say it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** It grieves us to say that the Hurds' root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mr. J. Pike was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. J. Pike has been busy with kindnesses this month; seven are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

> *The Almanac.* Eleven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and nine to Trevenna House. Market day was kept.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing crescent.*

**Reverses** We are sorry to report that the Pratt household has suffered heavy reverses; what was a comfortable house in the autumn is an anxious one now. The village's sympathy is sincere and its help is a matter for conscience.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. E. Hurd saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Poor Return** We regret to state that Mr. Josiah Trevenna, whom Mr. Jory Trevenna helped two weeks ago, has lately insulted Mr. Jory Trevenna at Trevenna House. The old saw about the hand that feeds is not new, but it is apt.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. A. Whitcomb sought a fight with Mr. E. Hurd at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. A. Whitcomb had planned.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**The Talk of the County** The tale that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" has grown in the telling, as tales do. It has crossed the river and come back larger.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. J. Pike has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

**Accounts Settled** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. Josiah Trevenna has paid Mr. L. Vance what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. Josiah Trevenna was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. L. Vance, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Through the Worst** It is agreeable to relate that the Stoughtons are through the worst of it. The ration is restored and the barrel, if not full, is no longer the subject of prayer.

**Ill Luck** It grieves us to say that a fever took hold of a child in the Dunleavys' house. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. The neighbors are doing what neighbors do.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**In Want** It is our unpleasant duty to note that the Dunleavys are in want, and have been for a day. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

> *The Almanac.* Thirteen matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market day was kept.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing gibbous, and the ford quiet.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Brenner house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "a fever took hold of a child in the Dunleavys' house" has reached every house on the Main Street, and some on the Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. E. Hurd, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. Jory Trevenna saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** It is agreeable to relate that Mrs. A. Hatch was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. Josiah Trevenna, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Hurds' root cellar went to rot" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

**A Poor Return** Mrs. A. Pratt is reported to have had a poor return for a kindness. Miss L. Pratt insulted Mrs. A. Pratt at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Sunday in the afternoon, four weeks after being helped by her. Memory in this county is long and not always grateful.

**Betrayed** A betrayal is reported. Mrs. A. Pratt, who was lent money to by Miss L. Pratt when it counted, has since came to blows with Miss L. Pratt. We withhold further comment until further is warranted.

**A Fight at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mrs. A. Pratt and Miss L. Pratt came to blows at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Sunday in the afternoon. Mrs. A. Pratt had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

**A Brawl at Hurd House** It pains us to record that Mrs. L. Trevenna struck at Mr. E. Hurd at Hurd House on Sunday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

**Hard Words at Brenner House** Hard feelings were aired at Brenner House on Sunday of an evening, Mr. K. Brenner doing the airing and Mr. J. Brenner the listening, unwillingly.

**A Brawl at Brenner House** Mr. J. Brenner sought a fight with Mr. K. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday of an evening and found one. The result was not what Mr. J. Brenner had planned.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Edwin Fenn was what the whispers said, and had been careful for years. The town learned nothing it did not already fear, and the fear did the rest.

**April 1.** The village was right, for the wrong reasons. Hester Fenn was of the hidden folk, and had been all along. It was not the rumors that found it out; it was coincidence, and a cow that died at the right time.

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ezra Sloat was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** It cost Lemuel Hatch more to pay than the sum was worth, and Gussie Pratt knew it, and took the money anyway out of respect.

**April 2.** Captain Hurd had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Five defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 3.** Johann Brenner was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Three defenses in a day, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 3.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Trevenna was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 3.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Gussie Pratt had been through them all in two days.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 866 meals eaten, 10 cold, about 2225 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on potatoes, salted butter and black tea. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** Magdalena Brenner left a half-sack of meal on Michael Dunleavy's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 8.** Nobody told Jory Trevenna that Abigail Hatch's pantry was bare. Jory Trevenna counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 9.** The louder Pastor Tveit spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 10.** It began as a kindness and ended as an obligation, which Johann Brenner resented in proportion to its size. What looked like treachery was, in his own account, merely escape.

**April 10.** Jory Trevenna's house was thin that week, and Konrad Strauss knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 12.** The anger was meant for someone larger. Hester Fenn was the one at hand. Johann Brenner was short with Hester Fenn for no reason that either of them could have given.

**April 12.** Bartholomew Pascoe left a half-sack of meal on Captain Trevenna's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 13.** It looked like generosity. In fact Mercy Bell could not bear to watch Gussie Pratt go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 855 meals (46 cold); stores ran short one times; the cellars lost clabber.

### Week 3

**April 15.** Nobody told Harriet Glover that Abigail Hatch's pantry was bare. Harriet Glover counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 15.** Reaction formation, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Pastor Tveit had been through them all in two weeks.

**April 15.** Karl Brenner would have named it betrayal. Johann Brenner would have named it self-respect. The record shows the kindness first and the injury after, a day apart.

**April 15.** Mercy Bell's house was thin that week, and Abigail Hatch knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 15.** Of the twelve good turns Captain Trevenna did in twelve days, not one was mentioned at the store. That was the point of them.

**April 16.** Clara Stoughton took ill of want of food in the thin season. The cause was on the table, so to speak: a winter of salt pork, meal, and nothing green.

**April 16.** Karl Brenner told Karl Brenner the same thing three times in two days, and did not believe it any better the last time.

**April 16.** The anger was meant for someone larger. Magdalena Brenner was the one at hand. Karl Brenner was short with Magdalena Brenner for no reason that either of them could have given.

**April 19.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Karl Brenner had been through them all in five days.

**April 21.** Behind the doors this week: 882 meals eaten, 61 cold, about 2226 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on fine milled flour, cornmeal and salt-cured bacon / side pork. Spoiled and wasted: nothing worth the naming.

**April 21.** Repression, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ingrid Tveit had been through them all in two weeks.

**April 29.** The Pascoe fortune turned by $29.60, to $66.60. The village credited the household's thrift. The household credited luck, and privately, a debt cleared and a cow that calved twice.
