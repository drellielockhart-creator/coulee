# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Bell, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. M. Brenner saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

**A Brawl at Glover Farm** A scuffle at Glover Farm on Sunday in the afternoon: Mr. H. Bell swung, and Mr. J. Brenner did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mr. M. Dunleavy was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Brenner, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: dry beans at 4¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. L. Trevenna, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. T. Glover saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mr. Jory Trevenna was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. A. Hatch, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. A. Hatch saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

**A Brawl at Schoolhouse** We are sorry to report that Mrs. M. Fenn struck at Mr. E. Hurd at Schoolhouse on Sunday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. D. Pascoe has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Brenner has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew nine to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** It pains us to record that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. M. Dunleavy were seen to quarrel at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening; the matter was a grave one, or looked it.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew eleven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing crescent; work laid by.*

**Reverses** We are sorry to report that the Pratt household has suffered heavy reverses; what was a comfortable house in the autumn is an anxious one now. The village's sympathy is sincere and its help is a matter for conscience.

**Talk** The loafers at the store maintain that Mr. J. Brenner is not altogether what he seems. The loafers are specific on nothing and certain of everything.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. A. Hatch has been busy with kindnesses this month; five are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Brawl at Brenner House** A scuffle at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon: Mr. J. Brenner swung, and Mr. K. Brenner did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

The difference between Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Josiah Trevenna was mended at Penhallow Diggings on Tuesday in the forenoon, or mended enough to share a bench.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**All Over the Ford** The story that "Hester Fenn is eaten up with envy" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot" has reached every house on the Main Street, and some on the Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing gibbous.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Brenner house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

**The Talk of the County** There is scarcely a door in Iron Ford where the story that "Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot" has not been told, and told again with additions.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**The Talk of the County** There is scarcely a door in Iron Ford where the story that "Hester Fenn is eaten up with envy" has not been told, and told again with additions. Miss H. Fenn has our sympathy, or our curiosity, as the case may be.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** We are sorry to report that Mr. J. Pike and Mrs. A. Pratt came to blows at Dunleavy's Tavern on Monday of an evening. Mr. J. Pike had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ezra Sloat was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** Captain Hurd had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Four defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** Johann Brenner was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Three defenses in a day, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 2.** A mind at its seams: repression again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Trevenna was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 4.** Isaiah Glover took ill of want of food. Not a sickness: arithmetic. The barrel had been divided too many ways for too many weeks.

**April 6.** The louder Ingrid Tveit spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 860 meals eaten, 10 cold, about 2216 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on potatoes, salted butter and black tea. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** Abigail Hatch's house was thin that week, and Jory Trevenna knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 10.** Minnie Fenn was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Four defenses in nine days, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 11.** Michael Dunleavy left a half-sack of meal on Lydia Pratt's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 11.** Captain Trevenna never told anyone what the kindness cost. It came off his own stores, and the table at home was the lighter by it.

**April 12.** Nobody told Harriet Glover that Ezra Sloat's pantry was bare. Harriet Glover counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 12.** Captain Trevenna's house was thin that week, and Konrad Strauss knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.
