# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. M. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mrs. M. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Bell, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. J. Pike, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. L. Trevenna saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mr. Josiah Trevenna was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. E. Fenn, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. D. Pascoe saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

**A Timely Kindness** It is agreeable to relate that Mrs. M. Bell was seen carrying relief to the door of Miss C. Stoughton, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

Several persons at Penhallow Row report the following: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**Threats at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. J. Brenner was heard to threaten Mr. L. Vance at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday of an evening. The threat was not specific as to means, but quite specific as to intent.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Fenn Farm: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

A curious report reaches us from Glover Farm: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. A. Whitcomb saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mr. J. Pike was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. J. Brenner saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. J. Pike has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing crescent, and the ford quiet.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Moe Farm. The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. A. Whitcomb has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mrs. S. Kvale was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. N. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. A second footfall sounds from inside the ledge.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** It pains us to record that Mr. J. Pike struck at Mr. J. Brenner at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. J. Brenner told Mr. M. Dunleavy what he thought of him at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday late at night, at a volume the whole room could appreciate.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Twelve matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**A Debt Pressed** We learn that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Mrs. M. Fenn for $2.40. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**Accounts Settled** It is agreeable to relate that Mrs. M. Fenn has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

**Accounts Settled** Mr. E. Fenn has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

**Accounts Settled** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. Jory Trevenna has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing gibbous; work laid by.*

**Talk** Whispers have begun to go about concerning Mr. J. Brenner. We do not repeat them. We observe only that they are going.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. D. Pascoe has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Brenner, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. L. Vance saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Glover Farm in the weekly register: Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** Mr. T. Glover sought a fight with Mr. J. Brenner at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Monday at noon and found one. The result was not what Mr. T. Glover had planned.

**Hard Words at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** Hard feelings were aired at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Monday at noon, Mr. J. Brenner doing the airing and Mrs. A. Pratt the listening, unwillingly.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** It was Ingrid Tveit who was said to be at fault. It was Nora Dunleavy who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 1.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ezra Sloat had been through them all in a day.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** Joel Pike's house was thin that week, and Michael Dunleavy knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 1.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Gussie Pratt had been through them all in a day.

**April 2.** Johann Brenner had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 3.** Michael Dunleavy left a half-sack of meal on Amos Whitcomb's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 790 meals eaten, 109 cold, about 2439 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on fine milled flour, potatoes and cornmeal. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

**April 8.** Nobody told Demelza Pascoe that Johanne Moe's pantry was bare. Demelza Pascoe counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 8.** Edwin Fenn had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Eight defenses in seven days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 9.** Captain Trevenna left a half-sack of meal on Bartholomew Pascoe's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 10.** Repression, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Minnie Fenn had been through them all in nine days.

**April 10.** Edwin Fenn could not bear a thing in his own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Demelza Pascoe. He would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 780 meals (85 cold); stores ran short once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 4

**April 22.** Hester Fenn could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Captain Hurd. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 22.** It passed through six mouths in a day, and grew a little in each. Nobody lied, exactly. Everybody improved it.

**April 22.** It cost Jory Trevenna more to pay than the sum was worth, and Gussie Pratt knew it, and took the money anyway out of respect.

**April 23.** The louder Pastor Tveit spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 26.** Nora Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Nora Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

### Week 5

**April 29.** Michael Dunleavy went to bed parched, and said nothing of it. A person can bear a good deal on the quiet.

**April 29.** Gussie Pratt went to bed parched, and said nothing of it. A person can bear a good deal on the quiet.

**April 30.** The anger was meant for someone larger. In Johann Brenner's mind it had found Nora Dunleavy instead, who had done nothing at all to earn it.

**April 30.** The debt was repaid on a Tuesday, without ceremony. It had been, for Konrad Strauss, a question of whether Jory Trevenna could be trusted; it was now a question of whether the answer mattered.
