# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** A fight took place at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening between Mr. J. Pike and Mr. J. Brenner. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mr. J. Pike left the field.

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mrs. N. Dunleavy sought a fight with Mr. E. Hurd at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mrs. N. Dunleavy had planned.

**A Debt Pressed** A correspondent informs us that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Mr. H. Bell for $3.80. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dry beans at 4¢ the lb and swiss cheese at 12¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the last quarter; work laid by. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. A. Hatch has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** A scuffle at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Sunday in the afternoon: Mr. H. Bell swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

Several persons at Dunleavy's Tavern report the following: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Blows were exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday of an evening: Mr. J. Pike against Mr. E. Hurd. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Three matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**A Fight at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** A fight took place at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Monday in the forenoon between Mr. M. Dunleavy and Mrs. A. Pratt. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mr. M. Dunleavy left the field.

A curious report reaches us from Dunleavy's Tavern: The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mr. Josiah Trevenna for $279.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Five matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing crescent.*

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855, under the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. H. Bell sought a fight with Mr. E. Hurd at Dunleavy's Tavern on Monday of an evening and found one. The result was not what Mr. H. Bell had planned.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Huldra's Shelf. Bells move through the bluestem without bending it.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. Three taps come from the stone after the last hammer-stroke.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

**Talk** It is the common report that Mr. J. Brenner is not altogether what he seems. The loafers are specific on nothing and certain of everything.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and five to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

High words passed between Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. J. Pike at Dunleavy's Tavern on Saturday of an evening; Mr. J. Brenner threatened Mr. J. Pike before others.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing gibbous; work laid by.*

**Unpaid** The debt of Mrs. A. Pratt to Mr. L. Vance, $215.00, stands unpaid twenty-eight days on. The book at the store has a long memory and a good hand.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. D. Pascoe has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Glover Farm in the weekly register: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot" has reached every house on Main Street, and some on Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** It was Ingrid Tveit who was said to be at fault. It was Nora Dunleavy who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ezra Sloat was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Gussie Pratt had been through them all in a day.

**April 2.** Captain Hurd had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Four defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** Johann Brenner was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Four defenses in a day, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 2.** The louder Hester Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 3.** Edwin Fenn had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in two days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 4.** Behind the door of the Whitcomb house: three days of eggs, fresh bread heel and green beans coffee, 0 cold meals, and nothing worth the naming lost to the damp. The village saw a quiet family and took it for a contented one.

**April 6.** Jory Trevenna needed help at the table, and Bartholomew Pascoe gave it.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 767 meals eaten, 37 cold, about 2349 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on potatoes, fine milled flour and cornmeal. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Fourteen defenses in seven days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 9.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 10.** The Whitcomb house came through the lean time on fine milled flour, green beans coffee and salt-cured bacon / side pork. Nobody at the store was told how near it had been.

**April 10.** Minnie Fenn was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Five defenses in nine days, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 776 meals (32 cold); stores ran short twice; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

**April 14.** Harriet Glover took time to help Captain Trevenna eat. The record cannot say what passed between them.

### Week 3

**April 18.** Hester Fenn had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Five defenses in two weeks, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 19.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 21.** Pastor Tveit formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their going. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Pastor Tveit walked past the gate afterward.

**April 21.** Ingrid Tveit formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their going. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Ingrid Tveit walked past the gate afterward.

**April 21.** Brigid Dunleavy took ill of want of food. Not a sickness: arithmetic. The barrel had been divided too many ways for too many weeks.

**April 21.** Behind the doors this week: 780 meals eaten, 44 cold, about 2404 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, potatoes and fine milled flour. Spoiled and wasted: clabber and whole milk.

**April 21.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Pastor Tveit had been through them all in three weeks.

**April 21.** It looked like generosity. In fact Jory Trevenna could not bear to watch Bartholomew Pascoe go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 21.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Bartholomew Pascoe was not well, and said otherwise.

### Week 4

**April 22.** The louder Minnie Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 22.** Lucius Vance went short at table long enough that it began to show: want of food. He ate last and least, and told nobody, and the household told nobody either.

**April 22.** Hester Fenn could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Kristoffer Moe. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 22.** Gussie Pratt wanted the money less than the acknowledgment. Horace Bell would have paid the one and denied the other.

**April 24.** It was Lydia Pratt who was said to be at fault. It was Edwin Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 26.** Michael Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Michael Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Nora Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Nora Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Brigid Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Brigid Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 28.** Week 4, as the larders knew it: 772 meals (47 cold); stores ran short once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 5

**April 29.** It looked like generosity. In fact Demelza Pascoe could not bear to watch Captain Trevenna go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 30.** The story needed no malice; it needed only six people each willing to be the one who knew. The truth, whatever it was, was never consulted.
