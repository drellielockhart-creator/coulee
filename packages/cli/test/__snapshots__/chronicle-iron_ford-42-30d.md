# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. J. Pike, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. M. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mrs. M. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Bell, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Debt Pressed** It is reported to us that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**Accounts Settled** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. Josiah Trevenna has paid Mr. L. Vance what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** A scuffle at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Wednesday at first light: Mr. H. Bell swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Eight matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew thirteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

Mr. E. Hurd and Mr. Josiah Trevenna were seen to quarrel at Trevenna House on Sunday in the forenoon; the matter was a grave one, or looked it.

**A Brawl at Glover Farm** We are sorry to report that Mrs. H. Glover struck at Mr. E. Hurd at Glover Farm on Sunday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**Threats at Dunleavy's Tavern** It pains us to record that Mr. J. Brenner used threatening language toward Mr. L. Vance at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday of an evening. We trust that cooler counsels will prevail.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**A Fight at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** A fight took place at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Saturday in the afternoon between Mrs. M. Fenn and Mrs. I. Tveit. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mrs. M. Fenn left the field.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Two matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing crescent, and the ford quiet.*

**Reverses** We are sorry to report that the Pratt household has suffered heavy reverses; what was a comfortable house in the autumn is an anxious one now. The village's sympathy is sincere and its help is a matter for conscience.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** It pains us to record that Mrs. N. Dunleavy and Mr. E. Hurd came to blows at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon. Mrs. N. Dunleavy had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

Mrs. I. Tveit and Mrs. M. Fenn made their peace at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Saturday in the afternoon.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing gibbous.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Brenner house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

**The Tveit-Fenn Difficulty** The Tveits and the Fenns are at open odds. We are sorry to report that three wrongs have been done and answered in two weeks, and nobody is willing to be first to forgive.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. T. Glover saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mr. K. Strauss was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "Captain Hurd has a violent streak" has reached every house on Main Street, and some on Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**Threats at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** Mr. J. Brenner was heard to threaten Miss L. Pratt at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Monday at noon. The threat was not specific as to means, but quite specific as to intent.

**April 29.** The story needed no malice; it needed only six people each willing to be the one who knew. The truth, whatever it was, was never consulted.
