# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mr. M. Dunleavy was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Bell, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. M. Brenner saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mrs. L. Trevenna was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. J. Pike, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** A scuffle at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Wednesday at first light: Mr. H. Bell swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Eight matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**Accounts Settled** Miss H. Fenn has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the last quarter; work laid by. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Brenner, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. M. Bell saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Brenner has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**A Fight at Penhallow Diggings** Blows were exchanged at Penhallow Diggings on Monday in the forenoon: Mr. Jory Trevenna against Mr. Josiah Trevenna. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. K. Moe were seen to quarrel at Dunleavy's Tavern on Monday of an evening; the matter was a grave one, or looked it.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

Several persons at Dunleavy's Tavern report the following: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

**A Debt Pressed** We hear from a respectable source that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Miss H. Fenn for $10.40. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

A curious report reaches us from Glover Farm: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**A Timely Kindness** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. A. Whitcomb was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. J. Pike saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mr. Josiah Trevenna was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Jory Trevenna made their peace at Penhallow Diggings on Thursday in the afternoon.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. J. Brenner saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**A Fight at Ferryman's House** Mrs. A. Hatch and Mr. E. Hurd settled a difference with their fists at Ferryman's House on Friday at noon. Mrs. A. Hatch was the settler.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. J. Pike has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Twelve matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mrs. M. Brenner has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing crescent, and the ford quiet.*

**In Want** Clara Stoughton's household are in want. We are sorry to report it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Ferryman's House. The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. A. Whitcomb has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855, under the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. Three taps come from the stone after the last hammer-stroke.

**A Brawl at Penhallow Diggings** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. Josiah Trevenna struck at Mr. K. Strauss at Penhallow Diggings on Wednesday in the forenoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing gibbous; work laid by.*

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Kvale Farm in the weekly register: A whitish spark holds at the root of the willow.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** It was Ingrid Tveit who was said to be at fault. It was Nora Dunleavy who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 1.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ezra Sloat had been through them all in a day.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** It cost Captain Trevenna more to pay than the sum was worth, and Lucius Vance knew it, and took the money anyway out of respect.

**April 1.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Gussie Pratt was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** Hester Fenn could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Demelza Pascoe. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 2.** Michael Dunleavy left a half-sack of meal on Amos Whitcomb's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 787 meals eaten, 113 cold, about 2429 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on fine milled flour, potatoes and cornmeal. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.
