# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. M. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mrs. M. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Bell, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** We are sorry to report that Mr. H. Bell struck at Mr. E. Hurd at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Wednesday at first light and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**Accounts Settled** Miss H. Fenn has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

A sharp exchange passed between Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. L. Vance at Dunleavy's Tavern on Monday of an evening; Mr. J. Brenner threatened Mr. L. Vance before others.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Three matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Brenner has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. J. Pike saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** A scuffle at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Friday in the afternoon: Mrs. A. Pratt swung, and Mr. J. Brenner did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dried apples at 10¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew sixteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. J. Pike has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

**Accounts Settled** We are glad to report that Mr. Jory Trevenna has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. A. Whitcomb was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing crescent, and the ford quiet.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. D. Pascoe has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**Accounts Settled** Mr. L. Hatch has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It pains us to record that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

**Talk** Whispers have begun to go about concerning Mrs. N. Dunleavy. We do not repeat them. We observe only that they are going.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. A. Whitcomb has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. L. Vance, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. L. Trevenna saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Debt Pressed** It is reported to us that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Miss H. Fenn for $11.90. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" has reached every house on Main Street, and some on Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ezra Sloat had been through them all in a day.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** The debt was repaid on a Tuesday, without ceremony. It had been, for Lucius Vance, a question of whether Captain Trevenna could be trusted; it was now a question of whether the answer mattered.

**April 1.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Gussie Pratt had been through them all in a day.

**April 2.** It was Demelza Pascoe who was said to be at fault. It was Hester Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 2.** Edwin Fenn was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Three defenses in a day, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 2.** Nobody told Michael Dunleavy that Amos Whitcomb's pantry was bare. Michael Dunleavy counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 787 meals eaten, 106 cold, about 2432 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on fine milled flour, potatoes and cornmeal. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** Repression, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ingrid Tveit had been through them all in four days.

**April 8.** Johann Brenner was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Twenty-one defenses in six days, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 8.** Hester Fenn had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Ten defenses in seven days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 8.** Johanne Moe's house was thin that week, and Demelza Pascoe knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 8.** Magdalena Brenner left a half-sack of meal on Tollef Haugen's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 9.** Jory Trevenna never told anyone what the kindness cost. It came off his own stores, and the table at home was the lighter by it.

**April 9.** Captain Trevenna had a name for it, kept to Captain Trevenna: not charity, but a debt to someone long dead.

**April 9.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 780 meals (77 cold); stores ran short twice; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 3

**April 16.** Jory Trevenna's house was thin that week, and Bartholomew Pascoe knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 16.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Karl Brenner was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 17.** Nobody told Konrad Strauss that Jory Trevenna's pantry was bare. Konrad Strauss counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 17.** It was Captain Hurd who was said to be at fault. It was Hester Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 17.** Minnie Fenn had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Twelve defenses in two weeks, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 18.** It cost Jory Trevenna more to pay than the sum was worth, and Konrad Strauss knew it, and took the money anyway out of respect.

**April 18.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 18.** Pastor Tveit was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Twelve defenses in two weeks, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 21.** Behind the doors this week: 784 meals eaten, 79 cold, about 2440 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, potatoes and fine milled flour. Spoiled and wasted: clabber and whole milk.

### Week 4

**April 25.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Eli Crane was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 25.** Amos Whitcomb left a half-sack of meal on Michael Dunleavy's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 26.** Michael Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Michael Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Nora Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Nora Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Brigid Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Brigid Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Lucius Vance formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Lucius Vance walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Captain Trevenna could not bear a thing in his own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Demelza Pascoe. He would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 26.** The louder Pastor Tveit spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 28.** Week 4, as the larders knew it: 779 meals (73 cold); stores ran short once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 5

**April 29.** It passed through six mouths in seven days, and grew a little in each. Nobody lied, exactly. Everybody improved it.

**April 30.** Michael Dunleavy went to bed parched, and said nothing of it. A person can bear a good deal on the quiet.

**April 30.** The story needed no malice; it needed only six people each willing to be the one who knew. The truth, whatever it was, was never consulted.
