# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**A Debt Pressed** We are informed that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Miss H. Fenn for $4.38. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dried apples at 10¢ the lb and swiss cheese at 12¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

Several persons at Dunleavy's Tavern report the following: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Josiah Trevenna made their peace at Penhallow Diggings on Thursday in the forenoon.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and eggs at 1¢ the piece. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**A Fight at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** A fight took place at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Saturday in the afternoon between Mrs. N. Dunleavy and Mr. Josiah Trevenna. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mrs. N. Dunleavy left the field.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. H. Glover has been busy with kindnesses this month; five are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Debt Pressed** Mrs. A. Pratt has pressed Mrs. M. Bell for 18¢, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

A curious report reaches us from Dunleavy's Tavern: The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** A scuffle at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Friday in the afternoon: Mrs. A. Pratt swung, and Mr. J. Brenner did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing crescent; work laid by.*

**A Debt Pressed** Report comes to us that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mr. Josiah Trevenna for $280.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** It grieves us to say that Mrs. N. Dunleavy struck at Mr. E. Hurd at Dunleavy's Tavern on Monday of an evening and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Huldra's Shelf. Bells move through the bluestem without bending it.

**Peace Made** A quarrel of some standing between Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Josiah Trevenna was ended on Tuesday in the forenoon. The village is divided between relief and disappointment.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. Josiah Trevenna has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**The Trevenna-Dunleavy Difficulty** Between the Trevennas and the Dunleavys the quarrel has taken root. It began, as such things do, in a small matter, and has since grown a large family of its own.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** We are sorry to report that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

**Peace Made** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. M. Dunleavy and Rev. A. Tveit have made up their difference at Iron Ford Lutheran Church; the hand was offered, and taken.

**A Brawl at Penhallow Diggings** Mr. K. Strauss sought a fight with Mr. Josiah Trevenna at Penhallow Diggings on Friday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. K. Strauss had planned.

**Hard Words at Penhallow Diggings** Language unfit for print was exchanged at Penhallow Diggings on Friday in the afternoon: Mr. K. Strauss called Mr. Jory Trevenna a name before witnesses, and the name has been going about the village since.

**A Brawl at Penhallow Diggings** It pains us to record that Mr. Jory Trevenna struck at Mr. K. Strauss at Penhallow Diggings on Friday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Twelve matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and five to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew five to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**A Debt Pressed** Mrs. A. Pratt has pressed Mr. Jory Trevenna for 31¢, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing gibbous, and the ford quiet.*

**Unpaid** The debt of Miss H. Fenn to Mrs. A. Pratt, $4.38, stands unpaid twenty-seven days on. The book at the store has a long memory and a good hand.

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Glover Farm in the weekly register: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

**Ill Luck** Ill luck is reported: goods went missing from the Dunleavys' keeping. We hear of nothing to be done but to bear it.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ezra Sloat was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Edwin Fenn had been through them all in a day.

**April 3.** Johann Brenner had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Five defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 3.** The louder Edwin Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 3.** It was Johann Brenner who was said to be at fault. It was Pastor Tveit who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 4.** Behind the door of the Whitcomb house: three days of eggs, fresh bread heel and green beans coffee, 0 cold meals, and nothing worth the naming lost to the damp. The village saw a quiet family and took it for a contented one.

**April 6.** Bartholomew Pascoe took time to help Jory Trevenna eat. The record cannot say what passed between them.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 767 meals eaten, 36 cold, about 2341 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, fine milled flour and potatoes. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** Gussie Pratt had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Five defenses in six days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 8.** A mind at its seams: repression again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Hester Fenn was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 9.** The louder Hester Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 9.** Rationalization, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Minnie Fenn had been through them all in eight days.

**April 10.** The Whitcomb house came through the lean time on fine milled flour, green beans coffee and salt-cured bacon / side pork. Nobody at the store was told how near it had been.

**April 10.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 775 meals (26 cold); stores ran short not once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 3

**April 15.** The louder Minnie Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 15.** It looked like generosity. In fact Magdalena Brenner could not bear to watch Edwin Fenn go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 18.** It was Kristoffer Moe who was said to be at fault. It was Hester Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 21.** Pastor Tveit formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their going. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Pastor Tveit walked past the gate afterward.

**April 21.** Ingrid Tveit formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their going. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Ingrid Tveit walked past the gate afterward.

**April 21.** Behind the doors this week: 765 meals eaten, 45 cold, about 2385 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, potatoes and fine milled flour. Spoiled and wasted: clabber and whole milk.

**April 21.** Pastor Tveit was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Four defenses in three weeks, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 21.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

### Week 4

**April 22.** Captain Trevenna never told anyone what the kindness cost in time and trouble.

**April 23.** Brigid Dunleavy went short at table long enough that it began to show: want of food. She ate last and least, and told nobody, and the household told nobody either.

**April 26.** Michael Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Michael Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Lucius Vance formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Lucius Vance walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Captain Trevenna could not bear a thing in his own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Demelza Pascoe. He would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 28.** Week 4, as the larders knew it: 767 meals (51 cold); stores ran short not once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 5

**April 29.** Bartholomew Pascoe went to bed starving, and said nothing of it. A person can bear a good deal on the quiet.

**April 29.** It looked like generosity. In fact Demelza Pascoe could not bear to watch Edwin Fenn go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 29.** Michael Dunleavy wanted only what was owed by an unknown hand: restitution. That was all. It was the most dangerous kind of wanting, because it was reasonable.

**April 29.** Nora Dunleavy wanted only what was owed by an unknown hand: restitution. That was all. It was the most dangerous kind of wanting, because it was reasonable.

**April 29.** Captain Trevenna confessed something to Jory Trevenna at Trevenna House. It is not written here what it was. It has not been mentioned since, and it has not been forgotten.

**April 30.** Captain Hurd's anger was meant for someone larger, and it settled on Konrad Strauss. Nothing was said, yet.

**April 30.** Konrad Strauss went to bed starving, and said nothing of it. A person can bear a good deal on the quiet.

**April 30.** Jory Trevenna went to bed parched, and said nothing of it. A person can bear a good deal on the quiet.
