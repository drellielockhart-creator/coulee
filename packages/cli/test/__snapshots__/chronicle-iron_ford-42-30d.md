# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern.*

**The Thin Spring** Short commons are the common lot on the ford this week. The Vances with the rest, and four other houses besides.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. T. Vik and Mr. E. Sloat settled a difference with their fists at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the small hours. Mr. T. Vik was the settler.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**In Want** We regret to state that the Vances are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

**In Want** The Fenns are in want. It is our unpleasant duty to note it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

**Peace Made** Mr. L. Vance forgave Mr. T. Vik at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening, handsomely and in front of people, which is the best way.

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 2¢ the lb and fine milled flour at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Court day and market day were kept.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a last quarter, and the ford quiet.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that a fever took hold of a child in the the Pascoes' house. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

**The Thin Spring** The Stoughtons are not alone: six other households are reported short of provisions, and the spring has hardly begun. It will be called the spring of the thin barrel by those who live to name it.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** We regret to state that Mr. T. Vik and Mr. E. Sloat came to blows at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the small hours. Mr. T. Vik had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**In Want** It is our unpleasant duty to note that the Stoughtons are in want, and have been for nine days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** A scuffle at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday in the small hours: Mr. E. Sloat swung, and Mr. T. Vik did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

**A Fight at Brenner's Smithy** A fight took place at Brenner's Smithy on Thursday in the forenoon between Mr. K. Brenner and Mr. J. Brenner. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mr. K. Brenner left the field.

**A Poor Aim at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. E. Hurd aimed a hard word at Miss B. Dunleavy at Dunleavy's Tavern and hit nothing; Miss B. Dunleavy was seen to smile, which is worse.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

**A Brawl at Brenner House** Mrs. M. Brenner sought a fight with Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner House on Friday at noon and found one. The result was not what Mrs. M. Brenner had planned.

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** Hard feelings were aired at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday of an evening, Mr. E. Sloat doing the airing and Mr. T. Vik the listening, unwillingly.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** It grieves us to say that Mr. T. Vik struck at Mr. E. Sloat at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday of an evening and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Ten matters of note this week. Posted at the store: fine milled flour at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market day was kept.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home.*

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Blows were exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the small hours: Mr. E. Sloat against Mr. T. Vik. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

Mr. L. Vance and Mr. T. Vik made their peace at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the small hours.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** A fight took place at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon between Mr. T. Vik and Mr. E. Sloat. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mr. T. Vik left the field.

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mrs. D. Pascoe told Mr. L. Vance what she thought of him at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon, at a volume the whole room could appreciate.

**A Coolness Between Neighbors** A coolness is reported between the Pascoes and the Vances. The latest word passed at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon, and was not a kind one.

**A Fight at Brenner House** Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. K. Brenner settled a difference with their fists at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon. Mr. J. Brenner was the settler.

**A Fight at Hurd House** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. J. Trevenna and Mr. E. Hurd came to blows at Hurd House on Sunday in the afternoon. Mr. J. Trevenna had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

**A Poor Aim at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. M. Dunleavy attempted to put an insult upon Mr. E. Sloat at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon, but misjudged the room. The laugh, we hear, went the other way.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** A scuffle at Dunleavy's Tavern on Monday in the afternoon: Mr. E. Sloat swung, and Mr. T. Vik did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

**A Poor Aim at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. E. Sloat aimed a hard word at Mr. T. Vik at Dunleavy's Tavern and hit nothing; Mr. T. Vik was seen to smile, which is worse.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Peace Made** A quarrel of some standing between Mr. L. Vance and Mr. M. Dunleavy was ended on Tuesday in the afternoon. The village is divided between relief and disappointment.

**A Poor Aim at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. A. Whitcomb attempted to put an insult upon Mrs. N. Dunleavy at Dunleavy's Tavern on Tuesday of an evening, but misjudged the room. The laugh, we hear, went the other way.

**The Hurd-Trevenna Difficulty** The Hurds and the Trevennas are at open odds. It pains us to record that six wrongs have been done and answered in two weeks, and nobody is willing to be first to forgive.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It pains us to record that the the Hurds' root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

**A Poor Aim at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. E. Hurd aimed a hard word at Miss B. Dunleavy at Dunleavy's Tavern and hit nothing; Miss B. Dunleavy was seen to smile, which is worse.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** Hard feelings were aired at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday in the small hours, Mr. E. Sloat doing the airing and Mr. T. Vik the listening, unwillingly.

**A Brawl at Brenner's Smithy** Mr. K. Brenner sought a fight with Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner's Smithy on Friday in the forenoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. K. Brenner had planned.

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** It is reported to us that a plain-spoken difference passed between Mr. A. Whitcomb and Mrs. N. Dunleavy at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday of an evening, the former giving the latter an insult that was heard by the whole room.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

**In Want** The Trevennas are in want. We regret to state it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

**The Long Hunger** For three weeks the pantry of the Stoughtons has been a thing to be measured by the spoonful. The county has known harder winters and does not remember them fondly.

**The Long Hunger** The Vances have now been short three weeks. The lean weeks have been very lean. We believe the worst is not yet over, and we know that the best is not yet in the ground.

> ♪ In the spring of fifty-five, down the Lantern Coulee,  
> ♪ The barrel was thin and the cellar was bare;  
> ♪ The Vances counted the last of the meal  
> ♪ And sang to the river a hungry air.  

> *The Almanac.* End of the week: twenty-five matters worth the printing. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept.

**The Whitcomb-Dunleavy Difficulty** It must now be called a feud. Four injuries have been given and returned between the Whitcombs and the Dunleavys in a day, the last at Dunleavy's Tavern on Saturday at noon, when Mr. A. Whitcomb gave Mrs. N. Dunleavy cause for complaint again.

**Peace Made** We are glad to report that Mr. L. Vance and Mrs. N. Dunleavy have made up their difference at Dunleavy's Tavern; the hand was offered, and taken.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It grieves us to say that someone has been at the Trevennas' smokehouse. The neighbors are doing what neighbors do.

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** Hard feelings were aired at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon, Mr. T. Vik doing the airing and Mr. E. Sloat the listening, unwillingly.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** A scuffle at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday late at night: Mr. E. Sloat swung, and Mr. T. Vik did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** It is reported to us that warm words passed between Mr. E. Sloat and Mr. T. Vik at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday late at night, the former giving the latter an insult that was heard by the whole room.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. T. Vik sought a fight with Mr. E. Sloat at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday late at night and found one. The result was not what Mr. T. Vik had planned.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

**Ill Luck** It pains us to record that the butter won't come in the Vances' churn. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "the the Hurds' root cellar went to rot" has reached every house on the Main Street, and some on the Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. T. Vik and Mr. E. Sloat settled a difference with their fists at Dunleavy's Tavern on Tuesday at first light. Mr. T. Vik was the settler.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** We are sorry to report that Mr. E. Sloat and Mr. T. Vik came to blows at Dunleavy's Tavern on Tuesday in the forenoon. Mr. E. Sloat had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

**A Betrothal** We are glad to report that Mr. T. Vik and Miss L. Pratt are betrothed. The minister has been spoken to; the date, we are told, depends on the roads and the crop.

**The Long Hunger** For three weeks the pantry of the Fenns has been a thing to be measured by the spoonful. The county has known harder winters and does not remember them fondly.

The difference between Mrs. N. Dunleavy and Mr. J. Pike was mended at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday in the afternoon, or mended enough to share a bench.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Blows were exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday in the forenoon: Mr. T. Vik against Mr. E. Sloat. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

**A Poor Aim at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mrs. N. Dunleavy attempted to put an insult upon Mr. A. Whitcomb at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday at noon, but misjudged the room. The laugh, we hear, went the other way.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** Ill luck is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. We hear of nothing to be done but to bear it.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. E. Sloat and Mr. T. Vik came to blows at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday in the forenoon. Mr. E. Sloat had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

Mr. L. Vance and Mr. T. Vik made their peace at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday of an evening.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Edwin Fenn was what the whispers said, and had been careful for years. The town learned nothing it did not already fear, and the fear did the rest.

**April 1.** The village was right, for the wrong reasons. Hester Fenn was of the hidden folk, and had been all along. It was not the rumors that found it out; it was coincidence, and a cow that died at the right time.

**April 1.** It was Lydia Pratt who was said to be at fault. It was Johanne Moe who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 1.** Ezra Sloat was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Four defenses in a day, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 1.** The debt was repaid on a Tuesday, without ceremony. It had been, for Gussie Pratt, a question of whether Pastor Tveit could be trusted; it was now a question of whether the answer mattered.

**April 1.** It cost Johann Brenner more to pay than the sum was worth, and Gussie Pratt knew it, and took the money anyway out of respect.

**April 1.** Nobody told Michael Dunleavy that Ezra Sloat's pantry was bare. Michael Dunleavy counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 2.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Tobias Vik was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 965 meals eaten, 176 cold, about 2271 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on potatoes, salted butter and black tea. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

**April 7.** Tobias Vik left a half-sack of meal on Lucius Vance's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.
