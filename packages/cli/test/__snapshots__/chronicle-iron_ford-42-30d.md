# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**A Debt Pressed** We are informed that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Miss H. Fenn for $4.38. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dry beans at 4¢ the lb and swiss cheese at 12¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**Unpaid** The debt of Miss H. Fenn to Mrs. A. Pratt, $4.38, stands unpaid five days on. The book at the store has a long memory and a good hand.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the last quarter; work laid by. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** A scuffle at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon: Mrs. N. Dunleavy swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

Several persons at Dunleavy's Tavern report the following: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Three matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

The difference between Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Josiah Trevenna was mended at Penhallow Diggings on Saturday in the afternoon, or mended enough to share a bench.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. D. Pascoe has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

A curious report reaches us from Dunleavy's Tavern: The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**A Fight at Penhallow Diggings** Mr. K. Strauss and Mr. Josiah Trevenna settled a difference with their fists at Penhallow Diggings on Tuesday in the afternoon. Mr. K. Strauss was the settler.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** It grieves us to say that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

Mr. Josiah Trevenna and Mr. K. Strauss made their peace at Penhallow Diggings on Friday in the afternoon.

The difference between Mr. Jory Trevenna and Mr. K. Strauss was mended at Penhallow Diggings on Friday in the afternoon, or mended enough to share a bench.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing crescent.*

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855, under the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Huldra's Shelf. Bells move through the bluestem without bending it.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Three matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew five to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. H. Glover has been busy with kindnesses this month; five are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing gibbous.*

**Talk** It is said about the village that Mrs. N. Dunleavy is not altogether what she seems. The loafers are specific on nothing and certain of everything.

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Glover Farm in the weekly register: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

**Ill Luck** Ill luck is reported: goods went missing from the Trevennas' keeping. We hear of nothing to be done but to bear it.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ezra Sloat was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Edwin Fenn had been through them all in a day.

**April 3.** Johann Brenner had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Five defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 3.** It was Johann Brenner who was said to be at fault. It was Pastor Tveit who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 4.** Behind the door of the Whitcomb house: three days of eggs, fresh bread heel and green beans coffee, 0 cold meals, and nothing worth the naming lost to the damp. The village saw a quiet family and took it for a contented one.

**April 6.** Jory Trevenna needed help at the table, and Bartholomew Pascoe gave it.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 779 meals eaten, 37 cold, about 2321 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, potatoes and fine milled flour. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.
