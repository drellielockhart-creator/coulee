# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** A correspondent informs us that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** Mr. H. Bell sought a fight with Mr. E. Hurd at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Wednesday at first light and found one. The result was not what Mr. H. Bell had planned.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**Accounts Settled** We are happy to record that Miss H. Fenn has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a last quarter, and the ford quiet. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

**A Fight at Penhallow Diggings** Mr. Jory Trevenna and Mr. Josiah Trevenna settled a difference with their fists at Penhallow Diggings on Monday in the forenoon. Mr. Jory Trevenna was the settler.

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; five are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

Several persons at Fenn Farm report the following: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Blows were exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday of an evening: Mrs. I. Tveit against Mr. J. Brenner. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** It grieves us to say that Mr. A. Whitcomb struck at Mrs. N. Dunleavy at Dunleavy's Tavern on Saturday at first light and was handled roughly for the trouble.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

A curious report reaches us from Glover Farm: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Peace Made** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Josiah Trevenna have made up their difference at Penhallow Diggings; the hand was offered, and taken.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

The difference between Mr. Jory Trevenna and Mr. B. Pascoe was mended at Penhallow Diggings on Thursday in the forenoon, or mended enough to share a bench.

**Threats at Dunleavy's Tavern** It grieves us to say that Mr. J. Brenner used threatening language toward Mrs. N. Dunleavy at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening. We trust that cooler counsels will prevail.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

> *The Almanac.* Eight matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

Mrs. N. Dunleavy and Mr. A. Whitcomb made their peace at Dunleavy's Tavern on Saturday at first light.

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** A scuffle at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Saturday in the forenoon: Miss L. Pratt swung, and Miss H. Fenn did not stay where she was put. Honors were not even.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing crescent.*

**Talk** Rumor has it that Mr. J. Brenner is not altogether what he seems. The loafers are specific on nothing and certain of everything.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mr. Josiah Trevenna for $277.80, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Ferryman's House. The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

**Peace Made** A quarrel of some standing between Mr. L. Vance and Mr. A. Whitcomb was ended on Monday at noon. The village is divided between relief and disappointment.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. Three taps come from the stone after the last hammer-stroke.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

**The Trevenna-Pascoe Difficulty** Between the Trevennas and the Pascoes the quarrel has taken root. It began, as such things do, in a small matter, and has since grown a large family of its own.

**A Brawl at Penhallow Diggings** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. K. Strauss struck at Mr. Josiah Trevenna at Penhallow Diggings on Friday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. L. Vance sought a fight with Mr. E. Hurd at Dunleavy's Tavern on Saturday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. L. Vance had planned.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing gibbous, and the ford quiet.*

**In Want** Clara Stoughton's household are in want. It is our unpleasant duty to note it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Kvale Farm in the weekly register: A whitish spark holds at the root of the willow.
