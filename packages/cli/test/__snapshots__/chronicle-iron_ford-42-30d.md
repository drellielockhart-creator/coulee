# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Bell, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. M. Brenner saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. M. Dunleavy was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Debt Pressed** It is reported to us that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Mr. J. Brenner for $6.40. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**Accounts Settled** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. J. Brenner has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

**Threats at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. J. Brenner used threatening language toward Mrs. A. Pratt at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Sunday in the afternoon. We trust that cooler counsels will prevail.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**A Brawl at Brenner's Smithy** It pains us to record that Mr. K. Brenner struck at Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner's Smithy on Tuesday in the forenoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

**In Want** We regret to state that the Whitcombs are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: dry beans at 4¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and market day were kept.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. E. Fenn, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. H. Glover saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the last quarter; work laid by.*

**A Timely Kindness** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. T. Glover was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. L. Trevenna, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of the Whitcombs is over; they held. The first ramps came, and the pig was a blessing, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

**Threats at Brenner House** Mr. J. Brenner was heard to threaten Mr. K. Brenner at Brenner House on Thursday at first light. The threat was not specific as to means, but quite specific as to intent.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and nine to Trevenna House. Market day was kept.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Brenner has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. L. Hatch saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Poor Return** Mr. J. Brenner is reported to have had a poor return for a kindness. Mr. K. Brenner insulted Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon, seven days after being helped by him. Memory in this county is long and not always grateful.

**A Fight at Brenner House** A fight took place at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon between Mr. J. Brenner and Mr. K. Brenner. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mr. J. Brenner left the field.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Bell has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. H. Glover has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Poor Aim at Brenner House** Mr. K. Brenner attempted to put an insult upon Mrs. M. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday of an evening, but misjudged the room. The laugh, we hear, went the other way.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. J. Pike was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Miss L. Pratt saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**In Want** The Stoughtons are in want. It grieves us to say it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that the Hurds' root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It grieves us to say that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

> *The Almanac.* Eleven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and nine to Trevenna House. Market day was kept.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing crescent.*

**Reverses** We are sorry to report that the Pratt household has suffered heavy reverses; what was a comfortable house in the autumn is an anxious one now. The village's sympathy is sincere and its help is a matter for conscience.

**In Want** It grieves us to say that the Dunleavys are in want, and have been for six days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mr. E. Hurd was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

**Through the Worst** It is agreeable to relate that the Dunleavys are through the worst of it. The ration is restored and the barrel, if not full, is no longer the subject of prayer.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of the Stoughtons is over; they held. The first ramps came, and the pig was a blessing, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

**Ill Luck** It is reported that a fever took hold of a child in the Dunleavys' house. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market day was kept.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing gibbous; work laid by.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Brenner house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "a fever took hold of a child in the Dunleavys' house" has reached every house on the Main Street, and some on the Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

**A Brawl at Hurd House** Mrs. L. Trevenna sought a fight with Mr. E. Hurd at Hurd House on Sunday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mrs. L. Trevenna had planned.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Edwin Fenn was what the whispers said, and had been careful for years. The town learned nothing it did not already fear, and the fear did the rest.

**April 1.** The village was right, for the wrong reasons. Hester Fenn was of the hidden folk, and had been all along. It was not the rumors that found it out; it was coincidence, and a cow that died at the right time.

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ezra Sloat was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** Lemuel Hatch paid Gussie Pratt in full and made a point of doing it in coin. Neither mentioned the months it had taken.

**April 2.** Captain Hurd had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Five defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 3.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Johann Brenner was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 3.** Captain Trevenna was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Five defenses in two days, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 3.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Gussie Pratt had been through them all in two days.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 868 meals eaten, 10 cold, about 2216 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on potatoes, salted butter and black tea. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ingrid Tveit was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 8.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 8.** Nobody told Jory Trevenna that Lemuel Hatch's pantry was bare. Jory Trevenna counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 9.** The louder Pastor Tveit spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 11.** Jory Trevenna's house was thin that week, and Bartholomew Pascoe knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 13.** Of the four good turns Mercy Bell did in twelve days, not one was mentioned at the store. That was the point of them.

**April 14.** Edwin Fenn took ill of want of food in the thin season. The cause was on the table, so to speak: a winter of salt pork, meal, and nothing green.

**April 14.** Minnie Fenn took ill of want of food in the thin season. The cause was on the table, so to speak: a winter of salt pork, meal, and nothing green.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 871 meals (9 cold); stores ran short no times; the cellars lost clabber.

**April 14.** Konrad Strauss left a half-sack of meal on Captain Trevenna's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.
