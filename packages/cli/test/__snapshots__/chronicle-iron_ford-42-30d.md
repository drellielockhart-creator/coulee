# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**A Debt Pressed** We learn that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Miss H. Fenn for $4.39. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**A Debt Pressed** Mrs. A. Pratt has pressed Mr. E. Hurd for $7.30, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** We are sorry to report that Mr. J. Pike and Mr. J. Brenner came to blows at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday of an evening. Mr. J. Pike had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dry beans at 4¢ the lb and dried apples at 10¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

Several persons at Dunleavy's Tavern report the following: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Two matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**Unpaid** Miss H. Fenn still owes Mrs. A. Pratt $4.39, and the matter is the subject of general remark. A debt, like a cough, tends to be heard before it is cured.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

**Unpaid** The debt of Mrs. A. Pratt to Mr. L. Vance, $215.00, stands unpaid fourteen days on. The book at the store has a long memory and a good hand.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. H. Glover has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

A curious report reaches us from Dunleavy's Tavern: The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**A Debt Pressed** It is reported to us that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mr. Josiah Trevenna for $280.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Blows were exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Tuesday of an evening: Mr. L. Vance against Mr. E. Hurd. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

Mr. J. Pike and Mrs. N. Dunleavy made their peace at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday of an evening.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** Mrs. A. Pratt sought a fight with Mr. J. Brenner at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Friday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mrs. A. Pratt had planned.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Ten matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** A scuffle at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Saturday in the forenoon: Mrs. H. Glover swung, and Miss H. Fenn did not stay where she was put. Honors were not even.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing crescent, and the ford quiet.*

**A Brawl at Brenner House** Mr. J. Brenner sought a fight with Mr. K. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. J. Brenner had planned.

**A Brawl at Glover Farm** A scuffle at Glover Farm on Sunday in the afternoon: Mrs. L. Trevenna swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Huldra's Shelf. Bells move through the bluestem without bending it.

**A Fight at Penhallow Diggings** A fight took place at Penhallow Diggings on Tuesday in the forenoon between Mr. K. Strauss and Mr. Josiah Trevenna. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mr. K. Strauss left the field.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. Three taps come from the stone after the last hammer-stroke.

The difference between Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Josiah Trevenna was mended at Penhallow Diggings on Wednesday in the afternoon, or mended enough to share a bench.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

**Talk** Rumor has it that Mrs. N. Dunleavy is not altogether what she seems. The loafers are specific on nothing and certain of everything.

**The Brenner-Whitcomb Difficulty** The Brenners and Amos Whitcomb's household are at open odds. We regret to state that three wrongs have been done and answered in three weeks, and nobody is willing to be first to forgive.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Nine matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and five to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew five to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing gibbous; work laid by.*

**Reverses** The Pratts have fallen on hard times, and fallen quickly. Creditors, we are told, are being polite. That is how one knows.

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Glover Farm in the weekly register: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that goods went missing from the Trevennas' keeping. The neighbors are doing what neighbors do.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ezra Sloat was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** Of the four good turns Michael Dunleavy did in a day, not one was mentioned at the store. That was the point of them.

**April 2.** Captain Hurd had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Four defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** The louder Hester Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 2.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Johann Brenner had been through them all in a day.

**April 2.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Four defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 3.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Edwin Fenn was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 4.** Behind the door of the Whitcomb house: three days of eggs, fresh bread heel and green beans coffee, 0 cold meals, and nothing worth the naming lost to the damp. The village saw a quiet family and took it for a contented one.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 768 meals eaten, 40 cold, about 2356 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, fine milled flour and potatoes. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** A mind at its seams: reaction formation again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Hester Fenn was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 8.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Gussie Pratt had been through them all in seven days.

**April 9.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Pastor Tveit was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 9.** Repression, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Minnie Fenn had been through them all in eight days.

**April 10.** The Whitcomb house came through the lean time on fine milled flour, green beans coffee and black tea. Nobody at the store was told how near it had been.

**April 10.** It looked like generosity. In fact Mercy Bell could not bear to watch Hester Fenn go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 10.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 776 meals (25 cold); stores ran short not once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 3

**April 16.** Captain Trevenna took ill of want of food. Not a sickness: arithmetic. The barrel had been divided too many ways for too many weeks.

**April 17.** Of the thirteen good turns Captain Trevenna did in two weeks, not one was mentioned at the store. That was the point of them.

**April 20.** The ration at Trevenna's fell to 90 in the hundred and stayed there. They told the church it was a choice.

**April 20.** Hester Fenn could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Johann Brenner. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 21.** Pastor Tveit formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their going. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Pastor Tveit walked past the gate afterward.

**April 21.** Ingrid Tveit formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their going. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Ingrid Tveit walked past the gate afterward.

**April 21.** Behind the doors this week: 773 meals eaten, 39 cold, about 2411 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, potatoes and fine milled flour. Spoiled and wasted: clabber and whole milk.

### Week 4

**April 22.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 22.** The louder Hester Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 25.** Lucius Vance went short at table long enough that it began to show: want of food. He ate last and least, and told nobody, and the household told nobody either.

**April 26.** Michael Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Michael Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Nora Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Nora Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Brigid Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Brigid Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Lucius Vance formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Lucius Vance walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** The louder Pastor Tveit spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 27.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Bartholomew Pascoe was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 28.** Week 4, as the larders knew it: 764 meals (44 cold); stores ran short not once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 5

**April 29.** The Trevenna house came through the lean time on what came to hand. Nobody at the store was told how near it had been.

**April 29.** Behind the door of the Brenner house, Johann Brenner tried it on with Karl Brenner: fight. It came to nothing, and the house went on with supper.
