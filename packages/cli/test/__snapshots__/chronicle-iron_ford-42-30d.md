# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** A correspondent informs us that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** A scuffle at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Wednesday at first light: Mr. H. Bell swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**Accounts Settled** Miss H. Fenn has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

**A Brawl at Penhallow Diggings** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. Jory Trevenna struck at Mr. B. Pascoe at Penhallow Diggings on Tuesday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

Several persons at Fenn Farm report the following: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Three matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

A curious report reaches us from Dunleavy's Tavern: The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

**Peace Made** Mr. B. Pascoe forgave Mr. Josiah Trevenna at Penhallow Diggings on Monday in the afternoon, handsomely and in front of people, which is the best way.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Six matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew sixteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

**Accounts Settled** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. L. Hatch has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** A scuffle at Dunleavy's Tavern on Saturday of an evening: Mrs. N. Dunleavy swung, and Mr. J. Brenner did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing crescent.*

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855, under the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**In Want** The Dunleavys are in want. It pains us to record it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

**A Poor Return** It grieves us to say that Mr. M. Dunleavy, whom Mr. A. Whitcomb helped two weeks ago, has lately insulted Mr. A. Whitcomb at Dunleavy's Tavern. The old saw about the hand that feeds is not new, but it is apt.

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** Language unfit for print was exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Friday late at night: Mr. M. Dunleavy called Mr. A. Whitcomb a name before witnesses, and the name has been going about the village since.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. A low note follows the scrape of a boot.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing gibbous.*

**Accounts Settled** Mrs. A. Pratt has settled with Mr. L. Vance, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Ferryman's House in the weekly register: A loop of red thread hangs from the pantry latch.
