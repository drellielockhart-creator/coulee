# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern.*

**A Debt Pressed** We hear from a respectable source that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mr. M. Dunleavy was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Debt Pressed** Mrs. A. Pratt has pressed Mrs. H. Fenn for $2.08, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**Accounts Settled** Mr. Josiah Trevenna has settled with Mr. L. Vance, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

A plain-spoken difference passed between Mr. J. Brenner and Mrs. M. Brenner at Brenner House on Tuesday at first light; Mr. J. Brenner threatened Mrs. M. Brenner before others.

Mr. J. Brenner threatened Mr. K. Brenner at Brenner's Smithy on Tuesday in the forenoon, and the village is not slow to remark it.

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**In Want** We regret to state that the Whitcombs are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Eleven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Court day and market day were kept.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. T. Glover saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mrs. H. Glover was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Fenn, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**Unpaid** The debt of Mrs. H. Fenn to Mrs. A. Pratt, $2.08, stands unpaid five days on. The book at the store has a long memory and a good hand.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the last quarter.*

**A Brawl at Brenner House** Mr. K. Brenner sought a fight with Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday in the forenoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. K. Brenner had planned.

**Accounts Settled** It gives us pleasure to note that Mrs. H. Fenn has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of the Whitcombs is over; they held. The first ramps came, and the pig was a blessing, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

**Peace Made** A quarrel of some standing between Mr. A. Whitcomb and Mr. M. Dunleavy was ended on Tuesday of an evening. The village is divided between relief and disappointment.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

The difference between Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Josiah Trevenna was mended at Penhallow Diggings on Thursday in the forenoon, or mended enough to share a bench.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-two to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market day was kept.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mrs. H. Glover has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home.*

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. A. Whitcomb saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It pains us to record that the Hurds' root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mr. J. Pike was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Brawl at Brenner House** A scuffle at Brenner House on Wednesday of an evening: Mrs. M. Brenner swung, and Mr. K. Brenner did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

> *The Almanac.* Five matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dry beans at 4¢ the lb and potatoes at 1¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing crescent; work laid by.*

**Reverses** We are sorry to report that the Pratt household has suffered heavy reverses; what was a comfortable house in the autumn is an anxious one now. The village's sympathy is sincere and its help is a matter for conscience.

**In Want** The Dunleavys are in want. We are sorry to report it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "Johann Brenner has a violent streak" has reached every house on the Main Street, and some on the Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Brenner has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Debt Pressed** We hear from a respectable source that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Mr. E. Hurd for $7.30. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mrs. N. Dunleavy and Mr. E. Hurd settled a difference with their fists at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon. Mrs. N. Dunleavy was the settler.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. A. Whitcomb has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

**Through the Worst** It is agreeable to relate that the Dunleavys are through the worst of it. The ration is restored and the barrel, if not full, is no longer the subject of prayer.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Hurds have had hard luck" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that a fever took hold of a child in the Dunleavys' house. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** Ill luck is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. We hear of nothing to be done but to bear it.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Twelve matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market day was kept.

**The Hurd-Dunleavy Difficulty** It must now be called a feud. Four injuries have been given and returned between the Hurds and the Dunleavys in nine days, the last at Dunleavy's Tavern on Saturday in the afternoon, when Mr. E. Hurd gave Mr. L. Vance cause for complaint again.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing gibbous.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Brenner house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**The Talk of the County** There is scarcely a door in Iron Ford where the story that "Johann Brenner has a violent streak" has not been told, and told again with additions. Mr. J. Brenner has our sympathy, or our curiosity, as the case may be.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Edwin Fenn was what the whispers said, and had been careful for years. The town learned nothing it did not already fear, and the fear did the rest.

**April 1.** The village was right, for the wrong reasons. Hester Fenn was of the hidden folk, and had been all along. It was not the rumors that found it out; it was coincidence, and a cow that died at the right time.

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 2.** Ezra Sloat had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Four defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** Denial, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Johann Brenner had been through them all in a day.

**April 2.** It was Thomas Glover who was said to be at fault. It was Edwin Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 2.** A mind at its seams: rationalization again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Hester Fenn was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** Hester Fenn could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Johann Brenner. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 4.** Michael Dunleavy left a half-sack of meal on Amos Whitcomb's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 790 meals eaten, 98 cold, about 2434 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, fine milled flour and potatoes. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

**April 7.** Gussie Pratt had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Eight defenses in six days, and the village was told it was nerves.

### Week 2

**April 8.** A mind at its seams: repression again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Trevenna was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 8.** Ingrid Tveit was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Eleven defenses in four days, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 8.** Repression, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Tobias Vik had been through them all in seven days.

**April 8.** Captain Hurd had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Fifteen defenses in seven days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 8.** Abigail Hatch's house was thin that week, and Jory Trevenna knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 9.** Rationalization, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Pastor Tveit had been through them all in eight days.

**April 9.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 9.** Minnie Fenn had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Nine defenses in eight days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 10.** Nobody told Konrad Strauss that Jory Trevenna's pantry was bare. Konrad Strauss counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 11.** Bartholomew Pascoe left a half-sack of meal on Captain Trevenna's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 12.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Karl Brenner had been through them all in seven days.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 785 meals (87 cold); stores ran short one times; the cellars lost clabber.

### Week 4

**April 22.** The story needed no malice; it needed only six people each willing to be the one who knew. The truth, whatever it was, was never consulted.

**April 22.** What neither house would say aloud: both were keeping count. Six wrongs each could recite, and each side's tally was the larger. The first of them, two weeks back, was a small thing that either could have undone with a word.

### Week 5

**April 29.** The Bell fortune turned by $19.07, to $41.55. The village credited the household's thrift. The household credited luck, and privately, a debt cleared and a cow that calved twice.

**April 29.** Michael Dunleavy went to bed parched, and said nothing of it. A person can bear a good deal on the quiet.

**April 29.** Repression, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Abigail Hatch had been through them all in three weeks.

**April 30.** Lemuel Hatch was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Three defenses in twelve days, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.
