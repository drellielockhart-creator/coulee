# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** We are informed that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mr. Josiah Trevenna for $281.80, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**A Fight at Penhallow Diggings** A fight took place at Penhallow Diggings on Tuesday in the forenoon between Mr. K. Strauss and Mr. Josiah Trevenna. The affair was brief, the language long, and Mr. K. Strauss left the field.

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**Threats at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. J. Brenner was heard to threaten Mr. M. Dunleavy at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening. The threat was not specific as to means, but quite specific as to intent.

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dry beans at 4¢ the lb and fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the last quarter; work laid by. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

**A Brawl at Brenner House** We regret to state that Mrs. H. Glover struck at Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

Several persons at Fenn Farm report the following: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Two matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

A curious report reaches us from Glover Farm: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It grieves us to say that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Three matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing crescent, and the ford quiet.*

**A Brawl at Brenner House** Mr. Jory Trevenna sought a fight with Mr. J. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. Jory Trevenna had planned.

**A Debt Pressed** We are told on good authority that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Mr. E. Fenn for $2.10. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855, under the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Glover Farm. The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. Three taps come from the stone after the last hammer-stroke.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Five matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing gibbous.*

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Ferryman's House in the weekly register: A loop of red thread hangs from the pantry latch.

**The Trevenna-Pascoe Difficulty** The Trevennas and the Pascoes are at open odds. We are sorry to report that four wrongs have been done and answered in four weeks, and nobody is willing to be first to forgive.

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** It was Ingrid Tveit who was said to be at fault. It was Nora Dunleavy who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 1.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ezra Sloat had been through them all in a day.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Gussie Pratt was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** Edwin Fenn had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** It looked like generosity. In fact Michael Dunleavy could not bear to watch Amos Whitcomb go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 4.** Behind the door of the Whitcomb house: three days of eggs, fresh bread heel and green beans coffee, 3 cold meals, and nothing worth the naming lost to the damp. The village saw a quiet family and took it for a contented one.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 781 meals eaten, 99 cold, about 2381 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on fine milled flour, cornmeal and potatoes. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** A mind at its seams: repression again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ingrid Tveit was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 8.** Johann Brenner had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Twenty defenses in six days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 8.** A mind at its seams: repression again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Hester Fenn was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 9.** Minnie Fenn had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Nine defenses in eight days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 9.** Nora Dunleavy was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Three defenses in eight days, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 10.** The Whitcomb house came through the lean time on green beans coffee, eggs and fine milled flour. Nobody at the store was told how near it had been.

**April 10.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 10.** Captain Trevenna never told anyone what the kindness cost in time and trouble.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 778 meals (90 cold); stores ran short not once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 3

**April 16.** It was Konrad Strauss who was said to be at fault. It was Edwin Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 17.** Of the ten good turns Jory Trevenna did in two weeks, not one was mentioned at the store. That was the point of them.

**April 17.** Rationalization, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Pastor Tveit had been through them all in two weeks.

**April 17.** Pastor Tveit could not bear a thing in his own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Johann Brenner. He would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 17.** Tollef Haugen had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Five defenses in thirteen days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 18.** It was Kristoffer Moe who was said to be at fault. It was Hester Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 18.** Joel Pike was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Three defenses in two weeks, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 18.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 21.** Behind the doors this week: 784 meals eaten, 75 cold, about 2407 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on cornmeal, potatoes and fine milled flour. Spoiled and wasted: clabber and whole milk.

### Week 4

**April 23.** The louder Captain Trevenna spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 23.** The louder Hester Fenn spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what she was afraid of.

**April 24.** Brigid Dunleavy went short at table long enough that it began to show: want of food. She ate last and least, and told nobody, and the household told nobody either.

**April 24.** Konrad Strauss never told anyone what the kindness cost in time and trouble.

**April 26.** Michael Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Michael Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Nora Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Nora Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Brigid Dunleavy formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Brigid Dunleavy walked past the gate afterward.

**April 26.** Lucius Vance formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at their hurt. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Lucius Vance walked past the gate afterward.

**April 28.** Week 4, as the larders knew it: 778 meals (62 cold); stores ran short once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.

### Week 5

**April 29.** A mind at its seams: repression again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Abigail Hatch was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 29.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Konrad Strauss had been through them all in nine days.

**April 29.** It was Lydia Pratt who was said to be at fault. It was Edwin Fenn who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 30.** Jory Trevenna remembered only the injury; Konrad Strauss remembered only the kindness. It is how two good memories make one bad quarrel.
