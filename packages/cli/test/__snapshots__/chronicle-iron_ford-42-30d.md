# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mrs. M. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Bell, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Brawl at Glover Farm** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. H. Bell struck at Mr. J. Brenner at Glover Farm on Sunday in the afternoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. M. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: dry beans at 4¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the last quarter; work laid by. Rain in the morning.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mrs. A. Hatch was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** A scuffle at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Sunday in the afternoon: Mr. H. Bell swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Brenner has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Bell has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. B. Pascoe struck at Mr. E. Hurd at Dunleavy's Tavern on Thursday of an evening and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

> *The Almanac.* Three matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and five to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew nine to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing crescent.*

**Reverses** We are sorry to report that the Pratt household has suffered heavy reverses; what was a comfortable house in the autumn is an anxious one now. The village's sympathy is sincere and its help is a matter for conscience.

**A Brawl at Brenner House** Mr. J. Brenner sought a fight with Mr. K. Brenner at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. J. Brenner had planned.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" has reached every house on Main Street, and some on Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**The Talk of the County** The tale that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" has grown in the telling, as tales do. It has crossed the river and come back larger.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

**Talk** The talk of the ford is that Mrs. N. Dunleavy is not altogether what she seems. The loafers are specific on nothing and certain of everything.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It pains us to record that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Eight matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing gibbous.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Brenner house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. D. Pascoe saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. D. Pascoe has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*
