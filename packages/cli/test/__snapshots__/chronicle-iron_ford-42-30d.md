# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. J. Pike, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. M. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Mrs. M. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Bell, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Debt Pressed** It is reported to us that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**Accounts Settled** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. Josiah Trevenna has paid Mr. L. Vance what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** A scuffle at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Wednesday at first light: Mr. H. Bell swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Eight matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew thirteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a last quarter, and the ford quiet. Rain in the morning.*

Mr. E. Hurd and Mr. Josiah Trevenna were seen to quarrel at Trevenna House on Sunday in the forenoon; the matter was a grave one, or looked it.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. D. Pascoe saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** Miss L. Pratt sought a fight with Miss H. Fenn at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Saturday in the forenoon and found one. The result was not what Miss L. Pratt had planned.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. A. Whitcomb was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**All Over the Ford** The story that "Johann Brenner has a violent streak" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. J. Pike saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. D. Pascoe has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** We are sorry to report that Mrs. A. Pratt struck at Mr. J. Brenner at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Friday at noon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing crescent.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Brenner, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. T. Haugen saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855. The first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. J. Pike has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**Hard Words at Dunleavy's Tavern** Language unfit for print was exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday of an evening: Mr. J. Brenner called Mrs. N. Dunleavy a name before witnesses, and the name has been going about the village since.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

**Talk** Whispers have begun to go about concerning Mr. J. Brenner. We do not repeat them. We observe only that they are going.

**Hard Words at Brenner's Smithy** Hard feelings were aired at Brenner's Smithy on Thursday in the forenoon, Mr. J. Brenner doing the airing and Mr. K. Brenner the listening, unwillingly.

**A Fight at Brenner's Smithy** Mr. K. Brenner and Mr. J. Brenner settled a difference with their fists at Brenner's Smithy on Thursday in the forenoon. Mr. K. Brenner was the settler.

**Accounts Settled** Mr. E. Hurd has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Twelve matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew sixteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew five to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**Accounts Settled** It is agreeable to relate that Mr. E. Fenn has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

**Accounts Settled** Mr. Josiah Trevenna has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

**Accounts Settled** We are happy to record that Mr. Jory Trevenna has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Poor Aim at Iron Ford Lutheran Church** Mrs. A. Pratt aimed a hard word at Mr. M. Dunleavy at Iron Ford Lutheran Church and hit nothing; Mr. M. Dunleavy was seen to smile, which is worse.

**Hard Words at Brenner House** Mr. J. Brenner told Mr. K. Strauss what he thought of him at Brenner House on Sunday in the afternoon, at a volume the whole room could appreciate.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*
