# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. M. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mrs. M. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Bell, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Debt Pressed** Report comes to us that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

**In Want** We regret to state that Amos Whitcomb's household are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**A Brawl at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop** A scuffle at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop on Wednesday at first light: Mr. H. Bell swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

**Accounts Settled** Miss H. Fenn has settled with Mrs. A. Pratt, and the book is closed. A debt repaid is a quiet event and a good one.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the last quarter; work laid by. Rain in the morning.*

**Accounts Settled** We are glad to report that Mr. Josiah Trevenna has paid Mr. L. Vance what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855; the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of Amos Whitcomb's household is over; they held. The first greens came, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

**In Want** The Tveits are in want. It is our unpleasant duty to note it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**A Brawl at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** It grieves us to say that Miss L. Pratt struck at Miss H. Fenn at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Saturday in the forenoon and was handled roughly for the trouble.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. Jory Trevenna has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

The difference between Mr. B. Pascoe and Mr. Josiah Trevenna was mended at Penhallow Diggings on Thursday in the forenoon, or mended enough to share a bench.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Five matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dried apples at 10¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. I. Tveit, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. N. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the waxing crescent.*

**Reverses** We are sorry to report that the Pratt household has suffered heavy reverses; what was a comfortable house in the autumn is an anxious one now. The village's sympathy is sincere and its help is a matter for conscience.

**Through the Worst** We are happy to record that the Tveits are through the worst of it. The ration is restored and the barrel, if not full, is no longer the subject of prayer.

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. A. Whitcomb sought a fight with Mr. E. Hurd at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon and found one. The result was not what Mr. A. Whitcomb had planned.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

**Talk** Whispers have begun to go about concerning Mr. J. Brenner. We do not repeat them. We observe only that they are going.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Brenner house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot" has reached every house on Main Street, and some on Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**In Want** It pains us to record that the Brenners are in want, and have been for seven days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

**Threats at Glover Farm** We are sorry to report that Mr. E. Hurd used threatening language toward Mr. T. Glover at Glover Farm on Monday of an evening. We trust that cooler counsels will prevail.
