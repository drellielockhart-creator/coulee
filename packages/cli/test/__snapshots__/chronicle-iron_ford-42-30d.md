# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mrs. M. Brenner was seen carrying relief to the door of Mrs. M. Bell, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. A. Whitcomb, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. M. Dunleavy saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

**A Debt Pressed** Mrs. A. Pratt has pressed Mr. J. Brenner for $6.40, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

**Accounts Settled** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. J. Brenner has paid Mrs. A. Pratt what was owing, to the last cent. The affair, which threatened to turn into something worse, has gone back to being a ledger.

**Threats at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters** It pains us to record that Mr. J. Brenner used threatening language toward Mrs. A. Pratt at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters on Sunday in the afternoon. We trust that cooler counsels will prevail.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. M. Dunleavy has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

**In Want** We regret to state that the Whitcombs are in want, and have been for three days. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Seven matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: dry beans at 4¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and fine milled flour at 5¢ the lb. Divine service drew fifteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Court day and market day were kept.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a last quarter, and the ford quiet.*

**A Timely Kindness** It gives us pleasure to note that Mrs. H. Glover was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. J. Pike, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of the Whitcombs is over; they held. The first ramps came, and the pig was a blessing, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

**A Brawl at Hurd House** Mrs. L. Trevenna sought a fight with Mr. E. Hurd at Hurd House on Friday of an evening and found one. The result was not what Mrs. L. Trevenna had planned.

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855. The waning crescent.*

**In Want** The Fenns are in want. It grieves us to say it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty-one to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Bell has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Timely Kindness** Mrs. M. Fenn, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. Jory Trevenna saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home.*

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**A Timely Kindness** We are happy to record that Miss L. Pratt was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**In Want** It is our unpleasant duty to note that the Stoughtons are in want, and have been for a day. The barrel sounds when tapped, and the store's book has been consulted more than once. It is no time for pride; the village will want to help, and ought.

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

**Through the Worst** We are glad to report that the Fenns are through the worst of it. The ration is restored and the barrel, if not full, is no longer the subject of prayer.

**Ill Luck** It pains us to record that the Hurds' root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855; the waxing crescent.*

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. M. Dunleavy, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mr. J. Pike saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for him.

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

> *The Almanac.* Six matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew nineteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eleven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and nine to Trevenna House. Market day was kept.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing crescent; work laid by.*

**Reverses** We are sorry to report that the Pratt household has suffered heavy reverses; what was a comfortable house in the autumn is an anxious one now. The village's sympathy is sincere and its help is a matter for conscience.

**A Timely Kindness** We are glad to report that Mrs. A. Hatch was seen carrying relief to the door of Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose table has been poor of late. These things are noticed, and recorded here for the credit of the place.

**First at Every Door** It is remarked that Mr. J. Pike has been first at every door where there is want. The lean weeks have shown who has a table to share, and who has a heart.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "the Hurds' root cellar went to rot" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

**The Talk of the County** The tale that "the Hurds' root cellar went to rot" has grown in the telling, as tales do. It has crossed the river and come back larger.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855, under the first quarter.*

**In Want** The Dunleavys are in want. It is our unpleasant duty to note it, and leave the rest to Christian charity.

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

**Through the Worst** The thin spring of the Dunleavys is over; they held. The first ramps came, and the pig was a blessing, and nobody was buried who might not have been.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It is our unpleasant duty to note that a fever took hold of a child in the Dunleavys' house. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**Sickness Abroad** A grave misfortune: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. The doctor has been sent for; the minister, we hope, need not be.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855. The waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Twelve matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb. Divine service drew twenty to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept.

**A Timely Kindness** Mr. Jory Trevenna, whose pantry has been bare, did not go hungry this week; Mrs. M. Fenn saw to that, and mentioned it to nobody. We mention it for her.

**All Over the Ford** By the best accounts the report that "the Tveits' best cow sickened and died" has reached every house on Main Street, and some on Ridge Road. Nobody will say who began it, and everybody knows.

**All Over the Ford** The story that "a fever took hold of a child in the Dunleavys' house" is now all over the ford. Whether true or not, it has reached those who matter least to refute it and most to repeat it.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855, the Lord's day. The waxing gibbous.*

**A Turn of Luck** Good news from the Brenner house: their circumstances are much improved, and their neighbors observe it with the mixed feelings that attend all good news.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. Full moon, and bright enough to read a letter by.*

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Edwin Fenn was what the whispers said, and had been careful for years. The town learned nothing it did not already fear, and the fear did the rest.

**April 1.** The village was right, for the wrong reasons. Hester Fenn was of the hidden folk, and had been all along. It was not the rumors that found it out; it was coincidence, and a cow that died at the right time.

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: displacement again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Ezra Sloat was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 2.** The debt was repaid on a Tuesday, without ceremony. It had been, for Gussie Pratt, a question of whether Lemuel Hatch could be trusted; it was now a question of whether the answer mattered.

**April 2.** Captain Hurd had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Five defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 3.** Johann Brenner was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Three defenses in a day, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 3.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Trevenna was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 868 meals eaten, 10 cold, about 2230 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on potatoes, salted butter and black tea. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

### Week 2

**April 8.** Nobody told Jory Trevenna that Abigail Hatch's pantry was bare. Jory Trevenna counted the chimney smoke and knew.

**April 9.** The louder Pastor Tveit spoke against it, the more it was plain to anyone who listened closely what he was afraid of.

**April 10.** Jory Trevenna's house was thin that week, and Konrad Strauss knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

**April 12.** Bartholomew Pascoe left a half-sack of meal on Captain Trevenna's step at dusk and was gone before the door opened. It was never mentioned, which was how both of them preferred it.

**April 13.** Mercy Bell had a name for it, kept to Mercy Bell: not charity, but a debt to someone long dead.

**April 13.** Minnie Fenn had stopped trusting her own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in twelve days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 861 meals (46 cold); stores ran short two times; the cellars lost clabber.

**April 14.** Minnie Fenn's house was thin that week, and Jory Trevenna knew it from the look of the washing. The gift came by the back door so no one need be thanked.

### Week 3

**April 15.** It looked like generosity. In fact Captain Trevenna could not bear to watch Captain Hurd go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 15.** Rationalization, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Gussie Pratt had been through them all in two weeks.

**April 16.** Clara Stoughton took ill of want of food in the thin season. The cause was on the table, so to speak: a winter of salt pork, meal, and nothing green.

**April 18.** Eli Crane had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Six defenses in seven days, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 21.** Pastor Tveit formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at drive them out. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Pastor Tveit walked past the gate afterward.

**April 21.** Ingrid Tveit formed a grudge against an unknown hand, and set its aim at drive them out. It was not mentioned. It was merely the way Ingrid Tveit walked past the gate afterward.
