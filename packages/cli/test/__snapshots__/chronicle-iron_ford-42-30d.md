# Iron Ford, Spring 1855: seed 42, 30 days

## Week 1: April 1 to 7, 1855

*Sunday, the 1st of April, 1855. The Sabbath under a full moon; the evening service will want no lantern. Rain in the evening.*

> *The Almanac.* A report from Glover Farm: The porridge bowl is licked clean on the outside.

Correspondents mention Brenner House. Three tools lie with their handles pointing to the threshold. We print the observation without deciding its cause.

**A Debt Pressed** We are informed that Mr. L. Vance has begun to dun Mrs. A. Pratt for $215.00. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 2nd of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

**A Debt Pressed** Mr. L. Vance has pressed Mr. Josiah Trevenna for $310.00, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

**A Good Neighbor** Mr. M. Dunleavy has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

*Tuesday, the 3rd of April, 1855. The full moon, which the Norwegians watch and the Cornish do not discuss.*

*Wednesday, the 4th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous. Snow in the morning.*

*Thursday, the 5th of April, 1855; the waning gibbous.*

*Friday, the 6th of April, 1855. The waning gibbous.*

*Saturday, the 7th of April, 1855, under the waning gibbous. Rain in the night.*

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, dry beans at 4¢ the lb and dried apples at 10¢ the lb. Divine service drew fourteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, six to Kvale Ridge Church and six to Trevenna House. Market drew six to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Court day and Market day were kept. Weather: snow on one day and rain on two days, the glass near 51 at noon.

## Week 2: April 8 to 14, 1855

*Sunday, the 8th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and the last quarter. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* Among this week's observations, at Iron Ford Sentinel Print Shop: The broom leans bristles upward against the shut door.

**A Debt Pressed** We learn that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Mr. H. Bell for $3.80. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 9th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

*Tuesday, the 10th of April, 1855, under the last quarter.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** Mr. M. Dunleavy sought a fight with Mr. J. Brenner at Dunleavy's Tavern on Tuesday of an evening and found one. The result was not what Mr. M. Dunleavy had planned.

*Wednesday, the 11th of April, 1855. The last quarter.*

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** Blows were exchanged at Dunleavy's Tavern on Wednesday of an evening: Mrs. N. Dunleavy against Mrs. I. Tveit. Two chairs and a good deal of patience were lost.

*Thursday, the 12th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

Several persons at Penhallow Diggings report the following: A second footfall sounds from inside the ledge.

*Friday, the 13th of April, 1855, under the waning crescent.*

*Saturday, the 14th of April, 1855; the waning crescent.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew eighteen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, eight to Trevenna House and seven to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 55 at noon.

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. M. Brenner has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

**A Fight at Dunleavy's Tavern** It is our unpleasant duty to note that Mr. J. Pike and Mr. E. Hurd came to blows at Dunleavy's Tavern on Saturday of an evening. Mr. J. Pike had the better of it. Neighbors parted them; the damage was chiefly to dignity.

## Week 3: April 15 to 21, 1855

*Sunday, the 15th of April, 1855. The Sabbath, and a new moon: a dark night for church-going and a dark one coming home. Rain in the morning.*

> *The Almanac.* The weather book has this beside Haugen House: Flour on the sill bears five narrow marks, all pointing inward.

A curious report reaches us from Glover Farm: The kindling is laid crosswise, smallest stick uppermost.

*Monday, the 16th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

*Tuesday, the 17th of April, 1855. The new moon: dark nights, and the old folks bar the door a little earlier.*

*Wednesday, the 18th of April, 1855. New moon. The coulee is black after sundown.*

**Ill Luck** We regret to state that Captain Hurd's root cellar went to rot. The older folk are not surprised, and say why, and are not asked to.

*Thursday, the 19th of April, 1855. The waxing crescent. Rain in the afternoon.*

*Friday, the 20th of April, 1855, under the waxing crescent.*

*Saturday, the 21st of April, 1855. The waxing crescent.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: the Tveits' best cow sickened and died. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

> *The Almanac.* Four matters worth the printing. Prices stood at the counter: cornmeal at 3¢ the lb, potatoes at 1¢ the lb and dry beans at 4¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, nine to Trevenna House and six to Kvale Ridge Church. Market drew eight to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters and two to Main Street. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on two days, the glass near 58 at noon.

**A Debt Pressed** Mrs. A. Pratt has pressed Mr. L. Hatch for $1.78, and in company. The account is long standing and the patience short.

## Week 4: April 22 to 28, 1855

*Sunday, the 22nd of April, 1855. Sabbath, under the waxing crescent; work laid by.*

**A Brawl at Dunleavy's Tavern** A scuffle at Dunleavy's Tavern on Sunday in the afternoon: Mrs. N. Dunleavy swung, and Mr. E. Hurd did not stay where he was put. Honors were not even.

**A Debt Pressed** A correspondent informs us that Mrs. A. Pratt has begun to dun Mr. E. Hurd for $7.30. Such matters are best settled before the neighbors discover them, which in this case they have.

*Monday, the 23rd of April, 1855; the first quarter. Rain from the morning on.*

> *The Almanac.* A traveler notes Glover Farm. The cream saucer stands beneath the bench, quite dry.

*Tuesday, the 24th of April, 1855. The first quarter.*

*Wednesday, the 25th of April, 1855; the first quarter.*

Our readers may judge the account from Penhallow Diggings. Three taps come from the stone after the last hammer-stroke.

*Thursday, the 26th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

**Ill Luck** It is reported that a fever took hold of Brigid in the Dunleavys' house. The superstitious have not been slow to remark that it is not the first such thing this season.

*Friday, the 27th of April, 1855; the waxing gibbous.*

**A Heavy Loss** A heavy loss is reported: someone has been at the Pascoes' smokehouse. Neighbors are asked to remember it when the subscription paper comes round.

*Saturday, the 28th of April, 1855, under the waxing gibbous.*

> *The Almanac.* Six matters of note this week. Posted at the store: potatoes at 1¢ the lb, cornmeal at 3¢ the lb and salt-cured bacon / side pork at 12¢ the lb. Divine service drew seventeen to Iron Ford Lutheran Church, seven to Kvale Ridge Church and seven to Trevenna House. Market drew seven to Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters. Market day was kept. Weather: rain on one day, the glass near 60 at noon.

**Peace Made** It gives us pleasure to note that Mr. H. Kvale and Mrs. A. Pratt have made up their difference at Pratt & Daughter, Outfitters; the hand was offered, and taken.

## Week 5: April 29 to May 5, 1855

*Sunday, the 29th of April, 1855. The Sabbath; a waxing gibbous, and the ford quiet.*

**A Good Neighbor** Mrs. A. Hatch has been busy with kindnesses this month; four are known to the village. The county could stand a dozen like.

> *The Almanac.* Entered beside Glover Farm in the weekly register: A neat plait has appeared in the loose straw by the door.

*Monday, the 30th of April, 1855. The full moon. The bluffs stand out white and the dogs are restless.*

## Epilogue: what the county never knew

*Set down after the fact, from the record of what was truly done, said, and felt. Nobody in the story had this.*

### Week 1

**April 1.** Johanne Moe could not bear a thing in her own heart and found it, quite sincerely, in Lydia Pratt. She would have been astonished to hear it called a defense.

**April 1.** It was Ingrid Tveit who was said to be at fault. It was Nora Dunleavy who needed it to be so: laying it at another's door, in the language of a later day, projection.

**April 1.** Displacement, and the others in turn: the mind has a drawer of such things, and Ezra Sloat had been through them all in a day.

**April 1.** A mind at its seams: denial again and again, and between them nothing to put in its place. Captain Hurd was not well, and said otherwise.

**April 1.** Captain Trevenna had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** Gussie Pratt was coming apart at a seam nobody could see. Three defenses in a day, and the relief wearing thin. The village took it for ill temper.

**April 2.** Edwin Fenn had stopped trusting his own reasons, and went on giving them. Three defenses in a day, and the village was told it was nerves.

**April 2.** It looked like generosity. In fact Michael Dunleavy could not bear to watch Amos Whitcomb go without, and that is a different thing, and a rarer.

**April 4.** Behind the door of the Whitcomb house: three days of eggs, fresh bread heel and green beans coffee, 3 cold meals, and nothing worth the naming lost to the damp. The village saw a quiet family and took it for a contented one.

**April 7.** Behind the doors this week: 784 meals eaten, 104 cold, about 2391 calories to the head. The town lived mostly on fine milled flour, cornmeal and potatoes. Spoiled and wasted: whole milk and packed dinner miner's crib.

**April 14.** Week 2, as the larders knew it: 776 meals (93 cold); stores ran short once; the cellars lost clabber and whole milk.
